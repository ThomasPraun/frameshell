Subtitles come from the transcript, so they follow every cut you make. They are a track on the timeline that follows a video or audio track.

## What to ask

- "Add subtitles."
- "Captions for a short: big, two words at a time, the key word in yellow."
- "Put the subtitles at the top for the vertical version."
- "No captions between 12 and 15 seconds, the title is there."
- "Show the speaker's name."

## Styles

- **Plain**: a classic subtitle line, for YouTube and long videos.
- **Big keyword**: large words, a few at a time, with the key word in an accent colour and a little bigger. Made for shorts and reels, where many people watch without sound.

Words can appear all at once, word by word, or letter by letter. The position can be top, center or bottom, and one format (say 9:16) can have its own position.

## In the app

Click a subtitle lane on the timeline to open its inspector and change the style. Click a cue to select its words. To fix a word, double-click it in the transcript view: the subtitle changes with it.

## Tips

- Proofread names, brands and numbers in the transcript first: captions show exactly those words.
- Keep captions below the chin and out of the platform's buttons. "Check the safe zones for TikTok" draws them over the frames.
- Captions are burned into the exported video.
