## What to ask

- "Put this track under the video, about 12 dB under the voice."
- "Make the music end exactly on the end card."
- "Here are three tracks: let me hear each under the picture."
- "Move the cuts onto the beat."
- "Duck the music while I talk."
- "I have no music: make a lo-fi bed for it."

## Land the ending

The most noticeable thing about music in an edit is how it ends. Mark the cut (for example the end card) and ask your AI to fit the track to it: the track slides so its own final hit lands on that cut. If you want the start to stay put, it can change the speed very slightly instead (at most 1.7 %, pitch kept).

## Pick by ear

Give two to seven tracks and ask for candidates. Each goes on its own audio track, at the same loudness, fitted to the same ending, all muted but the first. Right-click them in **Files** and choose **Audition the N music candidates…** to play them one by one at a matched level. Keep the one you like.

## The beat

Frameshell measures a track's beats and bars. With a tempo set, the timeline draws a beat grid, dragged clips snap to beats and bars, and your AI can place cuts, sounds and titles on `bar:8` or `beat:33` instead of seconds.

## Ducking

Ducking dips the music while someone speaks and brings it back in the pauses. After a later cut, ask to duck again: the keys follow the voice as it was.

## Music made for you

No track? Frameshell can make a music bed on your computer, with no licence to worry about: synthwave, lo-fi or minimal, with an intro, a build, a drop on a marker and a final hit. See [Sound effects](../sound-effects/).

## Licences

Music you bring from elsewhere needs a licence. Your AI records the source and licence of what it imports and lists the credits for your video description before export.
