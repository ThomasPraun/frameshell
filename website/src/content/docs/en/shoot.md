## Write the script

Give your AI your script in any form (a `.docx`, notes, an idea) and ask: "Write a shoot script from this." It writes a new file in `scripts/` and never touches your original. Each scene is a heading; each line says who speaks; notes like `[VISUAL: close-up]` are never read aloud.

For a podcast or talking points, ask it to check topics instead of every word.

## Record

Open **Shoot** in the sidebar.

1. Pick a **microphone** (the one thing a shoot needs), a **camera**, and if you want a **screen or window** and **system audio** (the sound of a call).
2. Click **Start shoot**. Every source records without stopping, one file each, until you click **End shoot**.
3. While recording, press <kbd>T</kbd> to start a take of the current scene and <kbd>C</kbd> to cut it. Each take is compared with the script.

Frameshell hides its own windows, the teleprompter included, from screen recordings while you record.

## The teleprompter

Open **Teleprompter** (from the sidebar, or in its own window on a second screen). It follows your voice as you read, and holds on lines marked as free talk. The first time, it downloads voice recognition once; without it, move it with the arrow keys.

## Takes

After each take, the Shoot view shows each scene as **Not recorded**, **To review** or **Done**, and marks every difference from the script: skipped lines, changed words. Pick the take you want per scene, or ask your AI to choose. Then ask for the first cut: it assembles the chosen takes on the timeline.

## After a call

When you recorded a call: **Split the call window into its tiles** makes one angle per person, **Find who speaks in the call audio** separates the voices, and **Link the speakers to the script's people** names them. Then cut it like any [multicam](../multicam/).
