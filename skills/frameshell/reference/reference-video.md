# Reference video

"Make it like this video" works when you measure the reference instead of guessing. `analyze` reads a video the person gives you (a reel they like, a competitor, their own older film) and writes down its **grammar**: how it cuts, what colours and look it has, how it moves, what happens when, how much text and graphics it carries, how loud it is, what tempo it keeps. `analyze diff` then measures how far our film is from it. Take the grammar; never take the content (characters, designs, script, footage). Commands assume the session rule of [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md).

## 1. Measure it

```sh
frameshell analyze references/reel.mp4 --json
```

MCP: `analyze { file: "references/reel.mp4" }` returns the sheets as images and the numbers as JSON. The video can be anywhere on disk (a download folder is fine); it is only read. A 1-minute 1080p video takes about 35 seconds (a background `analyze` job; MCP `job_list`). It writes `.frameshell/analysis/<name>/` (`<name>`: the file name in lower case; `--name` picks another; a new run with the same name replaces the folder; it is regenerable and never touches project files):

- `contact*.png`: a frame every 0.5 s (2 per second), 30 to a sheet, with the first frame of each shot marked `cut` (`4.00 cut`). Look at these first: they show the pacing, the layouts and where text sits.
- `shots*.png` and `frames/shot-<n>.jpg`: the middle frame of every shot, labelled `#<shot> <start>`. One JPEG per shot (640 px) to open when a shot matters.
- `analysis.json`: everything below, plus the beat lists (`tempo.beats`, `tempo.downbeats`).

## 2. Read the numbers

- **Cutting** (`stats`): `shotCount`, `cutsPerMinute`, `averageLength`, `medianLength`, `shortest`, `longest` (seconds). The median says more than the average: one long hold skews the average. A reel at 25 or more cuts a minute is fast; a talking head under 8 is calm. `shots` lists each shot's `start`, `end`, `length` and `cutScore` (how violent the cut that opens it is, 0 to 1).
- **Palette** (`palette`, and one per shot): up to 5 colours as `#rrggbb` with their `share` of the pixels. Black bars and a blurred background count as colours, so look at the frames before calling a palette the look. A palette that repeats from shot to shot is a colour system; one that changes is a mood change.
- **Look** (`look`, and one per shot): `brightness` (0 black to 1 white), `contrast` (spread of luma inside a frame, 0 to 0.5), `saturation` (0 grey to 1 pure colour) and `warmth` (-1 blue to 1 red; above 0 leans warm). The film's `look.grid` is 48 colours (8 columns, 6 rows, row by row from the top left): the layout of light and colour, e.g. a dark bottom third where captions sit. Each shot's own grid is in `analysis.json` (`shots[n].look.grid`). Use these to set a grade or a background, not to copy a picture.
- **Rhythm** (`rhythm`): `shotLengthCv` (spread of the shot lengths over their mean: near 0 is a metronome, above 0.6 irregular; compare it with the median), `stillShare` (share of frames in which the picture does not change: a talking head on a static background is high, a montage is low), `longestStill`, the big changes of the picture (`changeCount`, `changesPerMinute`: cuts plus flashes and jumps of the layout, so it is at least the cut count), `largestGap` (longest time with nothing big happening: where a viewer could drift away) and `bursts` (3 or more big changes within 1.5 s: where the reference accelerates). `review` (#457) is to report the same measures for our film on this scale: compare them as targets and say how far apart they are, not as pass or fail.
- **Events** (`events`, in time order, each with `startFrame` and `endFrame`): `flash` (the picture jumps brighter or darker for at most 0.25 s, `direction`, `amount`), `appear` and `disappear` (part of the picture gains or loses detail while the rest holds: an element enters or leaves, a caption shows or goes, but not over a subject that keeps moving, see section 3; `region` is its box as fractions of the frame on the 8 x 6 grid, which is where large text and graphics sit, and `area` its size), `hold` (the picture does not change for 0.5 s or more) and `hit` (a sudden rise of the sound: `level` in dBFS, `amount` the rise in dB, `offset` the seconds to the nearest big change of the picture within 0.2 s, negative when the sound comes first, null when none is near). A hit with an offset is an effect tied to a cut or an element; one with null is music or speech. A flash also opens and closes a shot at the default threshold: read it once, from `events`. Events are hints, at most 300 of a kind: look at the frames they name (the contact sheet, `frames/shot-<n>.jpg`) before telling the person.
- **Curve** (`curve`): how much the picture changes over time, `values` one per `step` seconds (0.1, longer for a film over 2 minutes), in grey levels on a 64 px wide picture: a cut is a spike, a held shot is a flat line. `moves` lists the steady stretches between the big changes (a slide-in, a pan, a zoom, a counter running up): `start`, `duration`, `peak`, and `ease`, the closest of `linear` (steady), `in` (starts slow, ends fast), `out` (starts fast, settles) and `inOut` (slow, fast, slow), with `fit` (0 is exact, over 0.5 a poor match: say "probably out" then). Moves over 3 s and under 4 frames get no ease. Slow moves that never pass the level of a still frame, the direction of a move and any overshoot are not measured: the change has no sign, so look at the frames. An ease that starts or ends very slowly shows only its faster 60 to 85 % as a move.
- **Ink** (`ink` for the film, `shots[n].ink`): how much of the picture graphics and text cover, as `mean` and `peak` share of the 8 x 6 grid (0 to 1), `peakAt` the first second of the peak and `region` the box of cells then. It sees detail that is not there at the shot's quietest, so a caption that shows for part of a shot is found and one that stays the whole shot is not (nor is a title card that is the whole shot). The box is whole grid cells (1/8 wide, 1/6 high), so it is where text sits and how much of the frame it takes, not the size of a letter: open the frame at `peakAt` to read it.
- **Sync** (`sync`): `offset` is the seconds the mouth of a talking face moves after the voice (negative: before it; positive means the picture is late or the sound early), to the frame, with `confidence` (0 to 1, how alike mouth motion and the loudness are). It needs a cached face scan of this exact file (`frameshell people scan <asset>` on a project copy of it: the scan is found again by content), so for a download it is mostly skipped, and `reason` says why: no sound, no scan, no face for 3 s, or no relation between mouth and voice (confidence under 0.2: another person is speaking, or music covers the voice). The mouth is read as picture motion in the lower part of the scanned face box (scans keep boxes, not mouth points), so a face that moves its whole head a lot reads noisier. Under 0.5 confidence, say "about" and check the frames.
- **Loudness** (`loudness`): integrated LUFS, loudness range in LU and true peak in dBTP. Compare with the project's `export.loudness` (default -17 LUFS) before copying a number: the reference may be mastered for a different platform.
- **Tempo** (`tempo`, null without a steady beat): `bpm`, `meter` and `confidence`. `low: true` means do not trust it: speech and ambient sound read as low, and calm music may read as double or half time (`alternatives` lists them). With music you want in your film, measure the track itself (`frameshell beats measure`), not the reference.
- **Times**: every time is a frame index ÷ fps rounded down to the millisecond (`startFrame`, `endFrame` give the frames). Use frames when you need to be exact.

## 3. What it does not find

A hard cut is found. A dissolve, a whip pan or a jump cut inside one continuous take is not (`--threshold 0.15` finds softer cuts and false ones; look at the frames it adds). Slow camera moves, a subject walking and a fade over more than 2 s are not events: they show in `rhythm.meanActivity` only. Text is located (`appear` regions), never read; over a talking head or any subject that keeps moving, captions and graphics that come and go give no `appear` or `disappear`: read them from the sheets. A file with a variable frame rate (a phone recording, a downloaded reel) is measured at its nominal rate, so its `stillShare`, `longestStill` and `hold` events may read too long. `look` of a shot that had no frame to read is named in `warnings` and is the nearest earlier shot's: not MEASURED. The direction and overshoot of a move, the size of a letter and a read of the text itself are not measured: read them from the sheets and say so. Mouth sync needs a face scan (see Sync).

## 4. Tell the person what you know and what you guess

Label every value you report. The result's `labels` does it for the values of `analyze` (a key is a field path and covers what is inside it; the most specific key wins; `frameshell analyze` prints the label before each line):

- **MEASURED**: read by `analyze` from the frames or the sound (shot times, cuts per minute, palette, look, shot-length CV, still share, bursts, the curve, LUFS, BPM).
- **ESTIMATED**: a heuristic's hint or seen by eye on the sheets (`events`, `ink`, a move's `ease`, `sync.offset`, where text sits, what is 2D or 3D, the camera move and its direction, where the hook and the twist land). Vision estimates of positions are approximate: name the exact frames to check (`#7 at 12.30 s, frame 369`).
- **DECISION**: our creative choice for the new film, or a choice of the run (`threshold`, `curve.step`).

End with about five decisions for an original episode ("cut every 1.4 s, median 1.2, CV 0.3; a burst of 4 changes at the hook; two-colour palette of the brand; -14 LUFS for this platform"), each saying which measured number it comes from. Never copy characters, designs or script. The numbers feed the style file and the plan of the new film: write them there, with the reference's file name as their source.

## 5. Compare our film with it

```sh
frameshell analyze diff exports/reel.mp4 references/reel.mp4 --json
```

MCP: `analyze_diff { file: "exports/reel.mp4", reference: "references/reel.mp4" }` returns the sheet as an image. It lines the two films up by their **activity curves** (how much the picture changes over time, correlated within `--max-lag` seconds either way, 3 by default) and then reads them at **matched times**, never the same clock. It is a distance to read, not a pass or fail: a different film in the same style scores low SSIM everywhere and can still match in energy. Writes `.frameshell/analysis/<name>/` (`diff.json` and `diff.png`; the default name is `<film>-vs-<reference>`, a new run of a name replaces the folder, so do not reuse the name of an `analyze` run you keep).

- **Alignment** (`alignment`): `lag` is the seconds to add to a time of our film to find the same moment of the reference (positive: the reference is later), to 50 ms; `correlation` of the two curves (above 0.5 they move alike); `source` is `activity` (found), `forced` (you gave `--lag`) or `none` (no clear match, 0 is used and a warning says so: give `--lag` when you know where the two start together). A static reference or film cannot be aligned.
- **Energy** (`energy`): mean and 95th-percentile motion of each over the stretch both have, their `ratio` (above 1 our film moves more, below 1 less: "our film moves 40 % less than the reference") and `windows`: both curves second by second, to find where one moves and the other holds.
- **SSIM** (`diff`): structural similarity of our frame and the reference's every `--every` seconds (0.5; a long stretch is read less often): `mean`, the lowest `min` and its `minAt`, and `below`, the share of readings under 0.5. Near 1 is the same picture, near 0 unrelated, negative inverted. A reference of another shape is stretched to ours (a warning says so), so a low SSIM may be the shape.
- **Sheet** (`sheets`): `--snapshots` pairs (8) of our frame left and the reference's right at the same matched time, the lowest-SSIM moments among them, labelled `film 3.20 ssim 0.91` and `ref 4.20`. Look at it before you say what differs.
- `labels` marks every value MEASURED, ESTIMATED (`alignment.lag`, `alignment.correlation`) or DECISION (`energy.step`, `diff.every`): say so when you report it, and give the activity gap as a target for the next cut ("the reference holds 2.1 s at 8 s where ours moves"), not as a score.
