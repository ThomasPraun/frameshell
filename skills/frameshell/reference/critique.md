# Critique loop

Look at what you built before the person does. A reel or a motion film is judged on the rendered file, by someone who did not build it, against a fixed rubric; then the worst problems are fixed and the loop runs again. Used by [reels.md](reels.md) and by any video you generate. Commands assume the session rule of [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md).

## 1. Make the evidence

Judge the **export**, never the project files or your memory of the code. Render a draft (`frameshell render --preset <preset> --quality draft --out exports/draft.mp4 --json`; a project with several formats: `--formats <id,id>` without `--preset`, and one set of sheets per file), then make the sheets of the file people will get with `review` (MCP `review`, which returns the images):

```sh
frameshell review --file exports/draft.mp4 --json
```

It writes `.frameshell/review/<n>/` (the last 10 runs are kept; project files are never touched) and prints each sheet's path, size and tile times; open the PNGs with your image tool. Every tile is labelled with its time in the file:

- `contact*.png`: a frame every 0.5 s (`--every` changes it), 30 to a sheet, several sheets for a long film. Review the timeline itself (no `--file`) and the sheet also marks clip starts and ends, cuts, markers, subtitle cues and the deepest zoom of a keyed clip (`1.50 cut`), which the file cannot tell.
- `phone*.png`: one frame per second at 360 px wide: how it reads on a phone.
- `motion-1.png`, `motion-2.png`: the two fastest moments (`peaks` in `--json` give their time and how much of the picture moved) as 12 consecutive frames: pops, overlaps and blank frames only show in consecutive frames.
- `seam.png`: the last 6 and the first 6 frames: does the loop jump.
- `beats*.png` (only with `--sheets beats`, on the timeline, not `--file`): one frame per beat of the tempo grid (`frameshell tempo set`; the grid `tempo show` lists), taken a quarter beat after the hit, labelled `bar N` or `beat N` like `bar:N`/`beat:N` times (up to 60). Does each cut, entrance or caption land on its beat? No tempo or a LOW-confidence grid writes no sheet and says why in `warnings`.
- `cuts*.png` (only with `--sheets cuts`): one row per cut, the frames 0.2 and 0.1 s before it, on it, and 0.1 and 0.2 s after (`1.00 cut 3 -0.1`), 6 cuts a sheet (a cut within 0.2 s of `--range`'s edge repeats the edge frame, so every cut keeps its row), up to 24 cuts (more are thinned evenly; `warnings` says how many were left out, narrow `--range` for them). A timeline's cuts are its clip edges on every visible picture track; a `--file`'s are the scene cuts `analyze` finds. Does the exit still move as the entry starts? Does a scale or a direction flip across the cut? Is there a flash frame or a black one?
- `safe-<platform>.png` (a vertical film, `--platform instagram|tiktok|shorts|youtube`): up to 36 frames with the platform's UI zones shaded red. A caption, a graphic or a face that reaches into a zone is marked `!subtitle`, `!graphic` or `!face` on its tile and listed in `safeZones[].flags` (`--json`) by subject, track, clip and zone with how many px it reaches in. A graphic is what a generated clip draws (titles included), measured on its alpha at up to 12 frames of the clip; a render with no alpha fills its frame, so a `warning` names the clip and it is not measured. A face comes from the cached `people scan` of the footage (`frameshell people scan` first: no scan, no flag); multicam shots are not placed. A graphic or a face in a zone is also a `safe-zone` craft warning. With a letterbox the film's bars cover graphics and faces that lie in them: they are measured inside `safeZones[].picture` (the rectangle the bars leave), captions on the whole frame since they are drawn over the bars. Look at the shaded bands yourself for anything else (text drawn into footage, a logo). A file has the zones but no flags. A vertical film whose `style.json` names platforms gets this sheet without `--platform`; `--sheets safe` alone uses those platforms too.

`--range 5,12` limits the sheets to those seconds; `--sheets phone,seam` writes only those; `--platform tiktok` adds the safe-zone sheet to the default ones. `--crop-at 0.5,0.8[,0.25]` crops every tile (not the safe sheet's) around one point of the frame, the last number the crop's share of the width, so a small detail reads: a cursor on a button, a foot on the ground; add `--clip <id>` and the point is in that clip's own picture and the crop follows it through its keyed moves and scale (`crop` on each tile in `--json` says what it shows). For a project with several formats review each format's file.

The per-clip kit, for one moment or one clip that looks wrong. Given without `--sheets`, a run writes only these (and the metrics with `--speed`, the safe-zone sheet with `--platform`), so it is quick; `--crop-at` crops `pop` and `onion` too (with `--clip`, both `pop` frames take the crop of the frame at `t`, so the difference shows the film, not the crop moving; `onion` follows the clip, so its ghosts show the motion inside the clip's own picture):

- `--pop <t>` (seconds or a marker, `hook+0.5`, `beat:8`), `pop.png`: the frame before `t` and the frame at `t` side by side, then their difference amplified 4 times (black where nothing changed). A pop is a bright patch in the third tile: what jumped, by how much. Not on the first frame.
- `--onion <clip>`, `onion.png`: up to 24 frames of the clip (of `--range` inside it) blended into one image: the still background, then each moving part laid over at 25 %. The spacing between the ghosts is the timing: even spacing is a linear move, spacing that tightens near a key is an ease into it, a gap is a jump.
- `--speed <clip>`, `speed.png` and `metrics.speed`: the speed of the clip's hero point per frame (film px per frame: the centre's travel plus half the change of its size), chart with linear stretches shaded amber and dead stops red. A footage clip's hero is its picture as its keys place it (position and scale, every frame, no render: `hero: placement`); a generated clip's is the centre and area of its render's alpha at up to 90 frames (`hero: alpha`; a render without alpha is `skipped` with the reason). `spans` lists each `linear` stretch (speed within 2.5 % for 6 frames or more and 20 % of the move: a key with no ease; an `in-out` ease is not one) and each dead `stop` (under 0.25 px per frame for 4 frames or more between two moving stretches: a hitch), ESTIMATED, warnings only. Ease the key (`clip key ... --ease out`) or make the hold intentional.

Then the numbers that are not pictures. `review` writes them too, to `metrics.json` (`metrics` in `--json` and over MCP), unless `--sheets` leaves `metrics` out:

- `picture`: `longestStatic` (the picture changes by under 0.5 grey levels, to the end of its last frame), `largestGap` between visual events (`analyze`'s big changes: cuts, flashes, jumps), `blank` stretches, `firstFrameEmpty` (frame 0 is the thumbnail). Measured on grey frames (Rec. 709 luma, so a change of colour alone counts) 64 px on the long side, the scale `analyze` uses: every frame of a `--file`; a timeline at most 90 samples over the range (`sampled`: the thresholds then apply per sample interval, and a flash shorter than the `spacing` can be missed). Judge a still stretch on the file.
- `rhythm`: `shotLengthCv`, `stillShare` and `bursts` (3 or more big changes within 1.5 s). On a `--file` they are MEASURED and are the numbers `frameshell analyze` prints for the same file, so set them beside a reference's `analyze` rhythm; on a timeline they are ESTIMATED from samples and the clip cuts of every track, so a title card on an upper track splits a shot: read a timeline's CV as an edit rhythm, not a picture-cut rhythm, and compare a reference with the rendered file.
- `subtitles.shortCues` (timeline): cues on screen for less than their words take to read (0.4 s for one or two words, else 0.75 x (0.5 + 0.3 s per word)).
- `loudness` (a `--file` only, after the AAC encode, which moves the peak): `integrated` LUFS, `range` LU, `truePeak` dBTP and `phoneBand`, the 200 Hz to 6 kHz band a phone plays. A timeline has no loudness: render a draft and review the file.
- `sync`: each sound against the picture event it belongs to. For a timeline, the generated sounds `sound place` put on it (the take's anchor, exact to the frame); for a `--file`, the hits found in its sound (a 12 dB rise in the 10 ms envelope). Whooshes, risers and ticks are left out (`excluded`): they do not land on an event by design. `offsets` gives, per sound, the nearest visual event within 0.12 s and `offset` = sound minus picture in seconds: a sound over 45 ms before its picture reads `early`, over 75 ms after it `late`, `none` with no event near. On a timeline the cuts and markers are events known to the frame (no widening); events found on the picture samples widen the limits by half the picture `spacing`: half a frame for a file, more on a sampled timeline. `near` is the share of sounds with an event within 0.1 s and `chance` the same share when the same sounds are moved by fixed offsets of 0.3 to 1.1 s (offsets inside the widened window are left out; `chance` is null when none is left): what random placement scores. A hit on every cut proves nothing, so read `near` against `chance`, then `silentCuts` (cuts of the timeline with no sound) and the sounds with verdict `none`; place sounds where something physical happens, never to fill a cut. Round placement so a sound is never before its picture.
- `seam`: `jump`, the mean grey difference (0 to 255 at 160 px) between the last frame and the first, with `verdict`: `duplicate` (under 0.5 while the picture moves, so the loop holds two frames), `continuous` (under 10) or `cut` (10 or more; a hard cut reads 40 to 80). It is the measure `render --loop` checks the file with ([loops.md](loops.md)). `--loop` (MCP `loop: true`) says the film repeats: a `duplicate` or a `cut` is then a `loop-seam` craft warning.
- `delivery` (`--file` with `--preset <preset the file was made with>`; `--timeline` and `--format` name the plan): the file read back with ffprobe against the export plan of the timeline with that preset: `width`, `height`, `fps`, `duration` (within 2 frames), video `codec`, `audio` and, for a file with sound, `loudness` against the preset's target (within 1.5 LU). Every check has `expected`, `actual` and `ok`; any `ok: false` is a `delivery` craft warning. The plan is the preset's own, made without `--quality`: a `--quality draft` file differs in frame rate by design, so run the check on the final render. Run it on every file you deliver.
- `sound`: the sound review list, the measurable half of "the voice is always on top, a sound only where something physical happens, no effect more than twice, the music resolves on the final frame" (you cannot listen: say "sound measured, not heard"). Review the **timeline** for it (no mix is needed: each clip is read from its asset's 10 ms envelope with its gain, keys and fades; dissolves are not crossfaded): `clips` counts the clips heard by role, `voice` (a `Voiceover` track, a track a `duck` names in `under`, an asset with a transcript), `music` (a `Music` track, a `sound music` take, a ducked clip), `sfx` (a `sound place` take, an `SFX` track) and `other`; footage on video tracks counts only as a voice. `voice`: per 0.5 s window where the voice speaks, voice minus bed (everything else) in dB, `minMargin`, `medianMargin` and `low` stretches under 6 dB (`voice-bed` warning: `duck` the music or lower the effects). `music`: the same windows against the music alone, judged by the style's `sound.musicUnderVoice` (`frameshell style show`; 12 dB by default, `from` says whether `style.json` sets it): `required` is that less 3 dB (never under 0) and `low` the stretches under it (`masking`: lower the music clip's `gain` or `duck --level`; both warnings on one stretch mean the music alone buries the voice). `edges`: clips that start with no fade inside sound their asset already plays before the in point, at -45 dBFS or more and 19 dB or more over the rest of the film just before (`edge-click`: `clip set --audio-fade-in 5`, or move the in point to a pause); a clip played from its asset's start, picking up the same sound or a dissolve is no cut-in. `repeats`: effect files heard more than once (ticks and clicks left out, key runs rotate them); over twice is `sfx-repeat`. `machineGun`: one file 4 times or more in a row, each within 1 s of the last (`machine-gun`: vary `variant` or `seed`; one take twice in a row is `sound place`'s own warning, not checked here). `chopped`: clips ending inside the film with their last 10 ms, as heard, above -35 dB of their loudest (`chopped`: `clip set --audio-fade-out` or end it after the sound dies; a 5 ms de-click ramp does not count, a clip continued by the same sound or a dissolve does not either; on talking-head edits every word-level cut of transcribed footage is checked, so expect some: fade them or waive with a reason). `ending` (the range must reach the end): `level` of the music on the last frame against its loudest and `musicEndsAt`; above -35 dB is `ending`: let the final hit ring out before the last frame (`music fit --end` at the end-card cut) or fade it (`music shape --ending`). For a `--file` the mix is all there is: `ending` is the RMS of the last 50 ms (above -45 dBFS: the film stops mid-sound) and `loudestAt`/`loudestShare` of the loudest 400 ms (after 85 % of the film it peaks at its end), and `clicks` lists bursts above 14 kHz over 9 times the RMS of their 20 ms with 80 % of it in 1 ms (`click`: a short audio fade at that edge), and `harsh` the 2 to 6 kHz band per 400 ms against that band in the loudest 400 ms (`reference`, at least 20 dB under the whole mix there): `spans` over 6 dB above it sound shrill (`harsh`: lower or swap the bright effect or music there). Run both: the timeline for voice, music and repeats, the file for clicks, harshness and its end.
- `craft`: each warning names its check (`static` over 3 s, `frame-0`, `blank`, `reading`, `peak` above -1 dBTP, `phone-band` 6 LU under the mix, `silent` over 15 s, `sync`, `loop-seam`, `delivery`, `safe-zone`, `voice-bed`, `sfx-repeat`, `machine-gun`, `chopped`, `ending`, `click`, `masking`, `edge-click`, `harsh`). Each has a `severity`: `warn` (fix it or say why the film wants it) or, only when the project says so in `requirements.json`, `fail`.
- `waived`: craft warnings a waiver of `requirements.json` covers, each with its `reason`. They are accepted, not gone: say so in the critique instead of dropping them.
- `requirements` (null without a `requirements.json` in the project root): what the person or the brief states the film must be, judged. Set it one value at a time with `frameshell review require <path> <value> [--clip <id>]` and `frameshell review waive <check> "<reason>" [--clip <id>]` (`--remove` takes a waiver back; MCP `review_require`, `review_waive`): each change is journaled like `style set`, so `history` lists it, `frameshell revert <op>` undoes it, and in review mode it waits in your proposal until the person accepts it. `review` never changes the file; a `file.write` or a hand edit of it is journaled too, and the JSON Schema is `requirements.json`. The parts, all optional: `film` (`film.maxSeconds`, `film.minSeconds`, `film.maxStatic` longest still stretch, `film.endCard` `{"minSeconds": 2}` a held, not blank last picture, `film.loudness` `{"target": -14, "maxTruePeak": -1}` with `tolerance` 1.5 LU, both on a `--file` only), `captions.minSize` (share of the short side, 0.026 is 28 px at 1080), per clip with `--clip` (`appearsBy` seconds or a marker like `hook+0.5`, `before` another clip's id, `keepsMoving` `{"maxStatic": 2}` the longest the whole picture may hold still while the clip plays, `staysInFrame` `true` or `{"margin": 0.05}`), `fail` (craft checks that fail in this project: `'["static", "reading"]'`) and waivers (the warnings of that check that overlap the clip, or all of them with no `--clip`). `staysInFrame` reads what the clip draws: a generated clip's alpha (a render with an alpha plane) or a footage clip's faces (a cached `frameshell people scan`), in the format reviewed (`--format`, after its cover crop, inside letterbox bars); touching an edge is leaving it, `margin` keeps that share of the frame clear on every side, and a failure lists the `spans` (timeline seconds) and the `format`. The alpha is read 160 px wide at up to 12 frames (4 a second): an element ending within about 1/160 of the width of an edge reads as touching it, and an exit shorter than the gap between those frames can pass unseen, so check a near miss on a `frame` and keep a `margin` for a graphic that only grazes the edge. Review every format you deliver: a clip can stay in the 16x9 frame and leave the 9x16 one. Each entry of `requirements.checks` is `pass`, `fail` or `skipped` (it cannot be measured here: a timeline has no loudness, a file has no clips, a clip draws nothing review can place) with `expected`, `actual` and a message; `requirements.failed` counts failed checks plus craft warnings of severity `fail`, and `frameshell review` exits 1 when it is not 0. A waiver with `matched: 0` is not needed or names a clip that is not on the timeline. Waive only with a reason the person would accept; a failed assertion is fixed in the film or by changing the requirement with the person, never by removing it quietly.

- **A moment `review` did not pick:** `frameshell review --range <a,b> --sheets motion`, or `frames_strip` (MCP) over the 0.5 s around an entrance or a cut with about 12 tiles.
- **Critical stills:** `frameshell frame --at <s> --out <file.png>` at frame 0, every graphic's entrance, each zoom peak, and the end card.
- **Words:** `frameshell transcribe --verify exports/draft.mp4 --json` when speech was cut.
- **Thumbnail:** a `warning:` from `render` that frame 0 is black is a hook problem; `frameshell cover --text <word> --json` reports whether the cover word reads at 180 px.
- **Delivery:** `frameshell review --file exports/draft.mp4 --preset <preset> --sheets metrics --json` and read `metrics.delivery`: size, frame rate, length, codecs and loudness against the export plan.

## 2. A fresh critic

The critic did not build the video. With subagents, start one and give it only the brief (or the person's request), the plan or shot list, the sheets, the stills and the numbers, never the code. Without subagents, do it yourself in the critic's voice: evidence first, no defending the work.

- **Describe before judging:** one sentence per contact-sheet frame, naming every object on it. An object that needs explaining is a problem in itself.
- **Measured beats seen:** vision judges layout and look well and fine motion and timing poorly. Timing, loudness and sync come from numbers (frame times, `review` metrics, word times); say which findings were measured and which only looked at ("sound measured, not heard": you cannot listen).
- **Unverified is not a pass:** a check you could not run is reported as not run.

## 3. Score or compare

**Score** a single version, 1–10 per criterion, each with evidence (timestamp, frame, number). 8 = ready for a demanding client.

| Criterion | Automatic cap |
|---|---|
| Hook: frame 0 already reads, the promise lands by ~1.5 s | Empty or near-blank frame 0 → max 6 |
| Readability at phone size (`phone.png`), the call to action the clearest text | CTA unreadable at 360 px → max 6; key text in the platform's UI zones → max 7 |
| Motion: weight, overlap, follow-through, nothing pops | A plain fade used as an entrance or exit → max 6; a visible pop or jump in a strip → max 7 |
| Pacing: something new every 2–4 s | A gap over 4 s, or a static run over 2 s outside the end card → max 7 |
| Brand and facts: real logos, UI and fonts, one accent; every claim sourced | Invented UI where real UI exists, a wrong font, a second accent, a claim with no source → max 6 |
| Sound: hits land with their picture, voice on top, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP | Hits off by over ~80 ms or loudness off target → max 6 |
| Composition in every format, the face free in talking-head video | A third of a 9:16 frame empty for over 1 s, or anything over the face → max 7 |
| Polish: no blank frames, no double-exposed text at swaps, clean seam | Any blank frame mid-film or double-exposed caption → max 7 |

A problem noted last round and still visible cannot score higher than last round.

**Compare** two or three versions when absolute scores feel arbitrary (choosing between variants, or checking that a round really improved): ask which is better per criterion, run it twice with the versions swapped, and count a version as better only when it wins both times. Each version must differ in something visible.

## 4. What to look for

Go through these one by one, on the sheets and strips:

- Frame 0 empty or black; the first word only readable after a few frames.
- A sound landing before its picture reads (visuals need a few frames to read: lead the motion, keep the sound on its beat).
- Text overlapping other text at a swap or a transition; a caption repeating what a graphic says.
- Everything fading in; everything moving at once (one hero move per moment); the same ease on every move; symmetric motion that looks mechanical.
- A shot that holds over one bar with nothing new; an end card that freezes; a loop seam that jumps.
- Text in the platform's UI zones; text under 28 px at 1080 wide; anything over the face or chin, also at the deepest zoom.
- Flat depth (cards like stickers); empty zones in vertical; type crossing busy UI while the camera moves.
- Banned looks: a centred title on a gradient, corner labels, frame borders, glow on UI, generic particles, crossfades between every shot, a whoosh on every cut, the same sound machine-gunned.
- Numbers or claims on screen the person or a source did not give.

Add every default you catch the video falling into to `banned` in the project's `style.json` with `frameshell style set banned '[…]'` (the whole list; with the person's agreement), so the next round and the next video avoid it. Judge brand, type sizes, motion and sound levels against `frameshell style show`, not your taste.

## 5. Notes, then fixes

Rank the problems by damage (P0: wrong or missing; P1: unreadable; P2: timing; P3: visual detail) and fix in that order. Write each fix as a scoped note:

```text
WHERE    12.4–13.1 s, the price card
PROBLEM  it enters 6 frames after the word "price" (measured from the transcript)
CHANGE   start the card 6 frames earlier; nothing else
KEEP     the zoom, the caption timing, every other graphic
```

Fix the three worst, each in its own transaction, then check each fix with a still or a strip of its range before re-rendering the whole film. Re-render drafts with `--quality draft`; only the delivery is `--quality final`. Append the round to `review/log.md`: the scores table (or the comparison), the three problems with their timestamps, what changed, and whether last round's fixes landed.

## 6. Stop

- **Reels:** every score 8 or more.
- **Motion films** made from a brief: every score 8 or more after at least three rounds.

Never show the person a film the loop has not passed, unless they ask for an early look. When you deliver, give the scores, the contact sheet and anything you know is still imperfect.
