# Fit a music track to the cut: back-timing, varispeed, drop shaping and candidates

#485 (epic #451) asks for `frameshell music fit <music clip> --end <marker or second>`: back-time a track so its own final hit lands on the end-card cut and plays unspliced before it, land exactly with a small varispeed, shape the music around a drop, and place several candidate tracks under the same picture at equal loudness. The owner decided option A on 2026-11-11 (the eight points below, every recommendation of the proposed decision accepted). It extends ADR 0029 (markers and beat grids) and ships in slices; **slices 1 (the shared TimeRef resolver), 2 (`music.fit`), 3 (loudness cache, candidates, `duck --level`) 4 (drop and ending keys) and the 5 ms `fadeIn` (as built after slice 4) are built so far.**

## Context (verified)

- Markers are built (ADR 0029 slice 1). The beat grid, `tempo.clip`, `--snap-to` and TimeRef names (slices 2 to 4) did not exist; no `TimeRef` symbol was in the code. ADR 0029 decision 7 gave that ADR the shared contract that voiceover (ADR 0034 decision 15) and sound placement also wait on.
- `track.duck` (`media/duck.ts`) writes `gain` keys marked `auto: "duck"` on the music clip's own clock, so moving or retiming the clip afterwards shifts them against the voice. The parameters of a duck are not stored.
- Media clips take `speed`; export applies `atempo` (pitch kept), the preview resamples linearly, so pitch shifts there until #499. Every audio segment already has a 2 ms edge fade; clips take `fadeIn`/`fadeOut`.
- Loudness is measured only inside export (two-pass `loudnorm`); there is no per-asset measurement. `EnergyStore` holds 10 ms RMS envelopes. The one FFT is private in `multicam/sync.ts` (ADR 0029 decision 9 moves it).
- `cleanup` is the precedent for propose-then-apply: `dryRun` and `skip`, one transaction, or one proposal in review mode (ADR 0019).
- #478: never depend on the `hyperframes` CLI; build in core what works on footage or the whole program. The epic's licensing note: no code, templates or text from reel kits.

## Decision

1. **TimeRef first.** `--end` takes seconds or a marker id or label, resolved in the daemon by the shared TimeRef resolver, the first piece of ADR 0029 slice 4. `beat:`, `bar:` and word refs join the same resolver later. `music.fit`, sound placement and voiceover placement use it; none gets a private parser (cross rule 2 on #451).
2. **Varispeed uses the existing clip `speed`** (export `atempo`). The preview pitch gap stays until #499. A tape-resample flag (pitch follows speed, `asetrate`) is added only if the slice 2 listening check finds `atempo` artefacts on music.
3. **±1.7 % is a hard limit, enforced by `music.fit`.** Above it the method refuses with the needed percentage and alternatives (move the end by a beat, trim the intro). No schema limit on `speed`, no flag to raise it in v1.
4. **Candidates (2 to 7) are separate audio tracks**, all muted but the first, matched by static gain on a new cached per-asset EBU R128 measurement (ffmpeg `ebur128`, keyed by content hash, a `gc` entry). The same cache backs `duck --level` (#489) and sound placement (#463). No timeline "variant" concept.
5. **Duck keys follow the fit.** When fit moves or retimes a clip that carries `auto: "duck"` keys, duck re-runs in the same transaction (its last parameters if stored, else the defaults) and `warnings` say so.
6. **Drop scope in v1:** gain keys marked `auto: "music"`: a step-down before the hit, a short dip after, and an ending on a bar line (once beats exist) or a 2 to 3 s fade after the last word. The rising high-pass build is a later slice (a swept-filter effect kind in the ADR 0031 bake, on the source clock). "Silence, then one soft note" stays a skill technique.
7. **The final hit** comes from `--hit <source seconds>`, else from the energy envelope; the result reports it with a confidence, and low confidence never varispeeds.
8. **Beat-grid moves wait for ADR 0029 slices 2 and 3.** No second grid inside #485.

One mutating method `music.fit` (`dryRun`, `skip`, like `cleanup`), one transaction `Music fit: …` (one proposal in review mode), writing only existing fields: `start`, `in`, `speed`, a 5 ms `fadeIn` on every entry, `fadeOut` and gain keys. Back-time: `start = end - (hit - in) / speed`; a track too short is reported, never looped or spliced. CLI group `music` is built-in and reserved, so plugins may not shadow it (cross rule 5); MCP tools come from the registry.

## Slices

1. **Shared TimeRef resolver** (seconds | marker), in core, adopted by the time arguments that exist today. Built, see below.
2. `music.fit`: back-time, `--exact` varispeed, dry run, CLI, MCP, skill, protocol bump, the measured `atempo` check on a synthetic percussive signal. Built, see below.
3. Per-asset EBU R128 cache, candidates, `duck --level`. Built, see below.
4. Drop and ending keys (`auto: "music"`; schema `auto` enum, JSON Schema regeneration). Built, see below; the 5 ms `fadeIn` followed with #585 ("The 5 ms `fadeIn` as built").
5. Grid moves, after ADR 0029 slices 2 and 3.
6. Swept high-pass build.
7. Tape-resample flag, only if slice 2 finds artefacts.

## Slice 1 as built (TimeRef)

- **Contract.** A time reference is a number of seconds, or a string tried in this order: numeric text; the id or label (ignoring case) of a marker of the timeline being edited; such a name followed by `+s` or `-s` (`hook+0.5`, `outro-1`); a scheme prefix. The whole string is tried as a name before the offset split, so a label like `end-card` is never read as `end` minus `card`. Marker labels are never id-shaped (ADR 0029 decision 2), so an id and a label never collide. `beat:`, `bar:` and `word:` are reserved: they are refused with a message that names what they wait for, and a built scheme joins by registering one resolver (`SCHEMES` in `time-ref.ts`). A marker labelled `beat:drop` still wins as a name.
- **Where it runs.** `resolveTimeRefs` (`packages/core/src/timeline/time-ref.ts`) runs in `TimelineService.apply`, in the operation's turn, against the timeline the operation applies to: the file, or the proposal timeline in review mode, so a marker added or moved earlier in the same transaction is seen. The engine and the journal only see seconds: a replay, a revert or an accepted proposal never depends on a marker staying where it was. An unknown name or a time before 0 fails as `InvalidOperation` naming the operation and the argument, and lists the markers.
- **Table.** `TIME_REF_FIELDS` (`packages/protocol/src/timeline.ts`) is the one list of the arguments that take a reference; the schema of each is a union of non-negative seconds and a name, and `ResolvedOperationArgs` gives the engine numbers. Adopted now: `transition.set` and `transition.remove` `at`, `clip.key` `time`, `clip.split` `at`, `cut` `from` and `to`, `clip.move` `start`, `clip.trim` `start` and `end`, `clip.add` `start`, `marker.add` and `marker.set` `at`. CLI: the matching flags (`--at`, `--start`, `--end`, `--from`, `--to`). Source-clock times (`in`, `out`) stay plain seconds.
- **Left for later.** The group-clock and other timeline-time arguments of `multicam.*`, `shot.*`, `track.duck` curves, `captions.set` `from`/`to`, `direct --from/--to` and the `ui` commands (`ui seek`) are not adopted; each joins by one line in the table plus its CLI flag. `beat:N` and `bar:N` joined the same resolver later (ADR 0029 slice 4 as built); word refs still wait for theirs. A name does not resolve in the app's own edits (they send seconds).
- Wire change: the params above accept a string; protocol 88.

## Slice 2 as built (`music.fit`)

- **Method.** `music.fit { clip, end, from?, hit?, exact?, dryRun?, skip? }` (CLI `music fit <clip> --end <time>`, MCP `music_fit`; protocol 101; the `music` group is built in, plugins cannot shadow it). `clip` is a media clip of an audio track. `end` and `from` go through the shared resolver (`resolveTimeRefArg` in `timeline/time-ref.ts`, the one that wraps `resolveTimeRef` for the operations): seconds, a marker id or label, an offset. Planned by `music/run.ts` (clip, hit, duck, room), arithmetic in `music/fit.ts` (pure), hit in `music/hit.ts` (pure); written by `daemon.ts` as clip operations in one transaction `Music fit: …` (one proposal in review mode), like `cleanup`.
- **Back-timing (default, `mode: slide`).** The clip keeps its speed and moves: `start = end - (hit - in) / speed` on the frame grid, so the hit lands within half a frame of `end` (`offsetMs`; the frame grid is the limit, `in` is snapped too). If that start is before `from` (default 0) the intro is dropped instead: the start is `from` and `in` moves later (`introCut`, written by `clip.trim` with `snap: false`, then `clip.move`); if the track is shorter than the picture before the hit the music starts late (`silentBefore`). Nothing is looped, spliced or stretched. The result reports `before`, `after`, `landsAt`, `tail` (music after the hit) and `steps`.
- **A second fit restores the intro.** Where the start lands after `from` and the clip plays the file from `in > 0` (an earlier fit dropped the intro, or `clip add --in`), `in` moves earlier, down to 0, until the start reaches `from`: a later end does not leave a gap that the file could fill. The result says so in a warning and a step; `silentBefore` is only what the file cannot cover. Operations: `clip.move` first to where the start sits before the head trim, then `clip.trim`, since a head trim that goes earlier would put the start before 0. In `exact` the move goes first when the start goes earlier, else `clip.set`.
- **`exact` (varispeed).** Keeps `in` and the start (the clip's own, or `from`) and sets `speed` so `start + (hit - in) / speed = end` (`clip.set`, then `clip.move` when `from` moves it). The limit of ±1.7 % is on the change of the clip's current speed (so a clip already at 1.25 may go to 1.25 ± 1.7 %), enforced here and not in the schema. Beyond it the refusal names the percentage needed, the window of ends that fit (frame-exact, `valid.end`) and the other ways: move the end, trim the intro, drop `exact`. A speed that already lands changes nothing.
- **The final hit.** `hit` when given (confidence 1), else the last sharp jump in the 10 ms energy envelope (`EnergyStore`, the one ducking and cut snapping read) inside the part the clip plays: a rise of at least 6 dB over the loudest of the previous 30 ms, to within 14 dB of the track's loud level, jumps closer than 100 ms merged. Confidence is the geometric mean of the sharpness of the jump (6 dB: 0, 18 dB: 1) and of how far the sound falls away 0.5 to 1.5 s after it (6 dB: 0, 24 dB: 1; a track that stops within 0.7 s of the hit scores 0.7), times closeness to the end of the played part (full inside 10 s, none from 30 s). A hit within 40 ms of a hit measured in a current `beats/<stem>.beats.json` (`onsets`, the beat detector's spectral flux) is placed on it. Under 0.5 the hit is only a suggestion: slide proceeds with a warning, `exact` refuses (`low confidence`), and `hit.candidates` lists the last strong hits to pick with `--hit`. A track with no jump near its loud level (a drone, a fade) has no hit: `no hit`, pass `--hit`. Measured on synthetic music (kicks every 0.5 s, a crash with a 2.5 s ring): placed to within one 10 ms hop, confidence 0.98; a track that stops on its last hit 0.84; a fade-out none.
- **Duck keys.** When the clip carries `auto: "duck"` keys and the fit changes it, `track.duck` runs again on its track in the same transaction (decision 5). The first duck's parameters are not stored, so it runs with the method's defaults (read from the registry, not copied) over every other unmuted video and audio track whose clips carry no duck keys; `duck.under` and `warnings` say which. `skip: ["duck"]` leaves the keys and warns; with no track to listen to it warns too. `skip` ids are the skippable `steps` of a dry run (only `duck` is, today), anything else is refused with the valid ones.
- **Checked before writing.** The new place may not overlap another clip of the track (a clear refusal before any operation, since the engine would stop half way); a clip that is not a media clip of an audio track, a hit outside the part the clip plays, an `end` not after the earliest start.
- **The 5 ms `fadeIn`: written later** (see "The 5 ms `fadeIn` as built"). When this slice was built no operation set a fade below a frame (`transition.set` rounds up to one frame, 33 ms at 30 fps, which is audible as a fade and not the edge click guard the ticket means), and the export already gave every audio segment a 2 ms edge fade.

### The `atempo` check (decides slice 7)

Measured on darwin-arm64 with ffmpeg 9.0.2 only; the test bounds are loose enough for other builds, but other platforms and ffmpeg builds were not checked.

Synthetic percussion (24 s at 120 BPM: a 60 Hz kick with 80 ms decay on every beat, noise snares on the backbeat, 8 ms noise hats between; `core/test/music-atempo.test.ts`), ffmpeg 9.0.2 on macOS arm64, 48 kHz, the kicks (about 45) measured on a 1 ms envelope (onset at 10 % of the peak, rise from 10 % to 90 %). `atempo` is the export's filter; tape resampling (`asetrate` then `aresample`, pitch follows speed) is what slice 7 would add.

| | onset against where the speed puts it | attack rise, mean (worst) | peak level, mean (worst) | length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| input | - | 3.1 ms (4.3) | -2.2 dBFS (-2.6) | - |
| `atempo` ×1.017 | **-19.6 ms** (-20.7 to -17.5) | 5.7 ms (19.0) | -2.5 dBFS (-3.5) | -20.9 ms |
| `atempo` ×0.983 | **-20.6 ms** (-21.5 to -18.1) | 5.6 ms (18.6) | -2.5 dBFS (-3.6) | -21.9 ms |
| tape ×1.017 | +0.5 ms | 3.0 ms (4.2) | -2.2 dBFS (-2.6) | 0.0 ms |
| tape ×0.983 | +0.5 ms | 3.1 ms (4.4) | -2.2 dBFS (-2.6) | 0.0 ms |

What it says:

- **`atempo` plays the material about 21 ms early, the same for every hit** (the spread across the file is 3 ms), so it does not drift; it is the start-up of the filter (21 ms is 1024 samples at 48 kHz). The chain the compiler builds (`atrim`, `atempo`, `apad`, `atrim`) shows the same: a click at 1 s lands 20.9 ms early at ×1.017 and 21.9 ms at ×0.983 (13 ms at ×1.25, 12 ms at ×2). The preview resamples linearly and has no such shift. So in the export a varisped hit lands a fraction of a frame (0.6 at 30 fps) before `end`, and the two disagree by that much for any clip with a `speed`, not only a fitted one. `music.fit` warns about it in `exact` mode.
- **`atempo` softens attacks**: the mean rise of a kick doubles (3.1 to 5.7 ms), a few worst ones to 19 ms, and the mean peak drops 0.35 dB (worst 1.3 dB). Small, and masked in a mix, but measurable on a bare kick.
- **Tape resampling is clean**: +0.5 ms, the attack and peak unchanged, the length exact. Its cost is the pitch, 0.29 semitone at 1.7 %.
- **Reading for slice 7.** The artefacts are measurable but inaudible by the numbers (a sub-frame, constant shift, 2.6 ms of attack, 0.35 dB). The shift is the one that matters for "lands exactly", and it has a cheaper fix than a second speed semantics: pre-roll in the compiler chain, a change that moves the golden plans and the export of every sped-up clip, to be decided with #499 (the preview's stretch). Recommendation: do not add the tape flag for now; fix the shift in the compiler with #499, and listen to a real track before adding any schema field. Owner's call.

## Slice 3 as built (loudness cache, candidates, `duck --level`)

- **The cache.** `LoudnessStore` (`media/loudness-store.ts`) reads the EBU R128 loudness of a whole asset (integrated LUFS, range, true peak) with the one `measureLoudness` the reference analysis already uses (ffmpeg `ebur128` over the file itself, so it needs no sidecar). Keyed by content hash and a version (`<hash20>-l1.json` in `.frameshell/loudness/`), with an in-memory layer; silence is a reading with `integrated: null`, a file with no sound is `InvalidOperation`, a missing ffmpeg fails as the `Binary*` errors. `gc` sweeps it by content like the energy envelopes (kind `loudness`, temps by age). The gain arithmetic is pure, in `media/loudness-plan.ts` (`gainFor` to 0.1 dB, `voiceLoudness`, `candidateTarget`, `peakWarning`), and is the one place sound placement (#463) should call for a level in dB under the voice. The measurement is of the whole file at its own level, not of the part a clip plays: a track whose played part is louder or quieter than its average sits off by that much (the skill says check by ear). Measuring the played range would be a second, uncached measurement; left for when a case needs it.
- **`music.candidates { assets, end?, from?, loudness?, dryRun? }`** (CLI `music candidates <asset> <asset>…`, MCP `music_candidates`; protocol 110). 2 to 7 distinct files. Each goes on its own new audio track `Music <rank>`, all muted but rank 1, with a static `audio.gain` that brings the file to one target: `loudness` (LUFS) when given, else the quietest placed file's own loudness, so nothing is raised and nothing newly clips; a raise (a given target above a file) warns when the true peak would pass -1 dBTP, the export's ceiling. The clip starts at `from` (default 0) and plays the whole file. With `end` each is fitted by `planMusicFit` as a slide, on a throw-away audio track in a copy of the timeline (so the hit finder, the intro drop, the late start and every warning are `music.fit`'s own, not a twin), and written as one `clip.add` at the fitted `start` and `in`. **Ranking** (only with `end`): files whose picture before the hit is covered first (`silentBefore` 0), then by the confidence of the final hit, ties in the order given; without `end` the order given is the rank. A file that cannot be placed (silent, no sound, no final hit, undecodable) is in `skipped` and the others go on; none placeable, a file twice, or `end` not after `from` is `InvalidOperation` before anything is written. One standalone transaction `Music candidates: <n> tracks at <LUFS> LUFS` (`track.add`, `track.set` muted and `clip.add` per candidate; one proposal in review mode), checked against a timeline that changed while it was planned (`StaleRevision`). Choosing stays two existing commands (`track set --unmuted`, `track remove --force`); `music.pick` is still later.
- **`track.duck` `level`** (CLI `duck --level dB`). dB the music's integrated loudness sits under the voice's. The voice is every unmuted media clip of the `under` tracks: its file's integrated loudness plus its static gain, energy-averaged by the seconds it plays, so a short aside does not outweigh the main voice. Each media clip of the music track gets `audio.gain = target - its loudness` (0.1 dB), then the dip is taken from that level (the duck already reads `audio.gain`), all in one transaction `Duck music: <track> <level> dB under the voice` (`clip.set`s, then `track.duck`). A clip with gain keys of its own (not duck keys) is left alone and named; none settable, or no voice to measure, is refused before writing. The result's `level` gives the voice, the target and each clip's loudness and gain. Without `level` nothing changes about gains.
- **The duck settings are stored (decision 5).** `track.duck` writes what it was asked (`under`, `depth`, `attackMs`, `releaseMs`, `threshold`, `gap`, `level`) into `audio.duck` of every clip that ends with duck keys, and removes it from a clip it no longer ducks; the operation takes them as an optional `settings`. They sit on the clip, not the track, because a track field is a header change and patches replace a whole track: `changes.updated`, history diffs and review rebase would then name the track instead of the clips the duck wrote. `music.fit` re-runs the duck with the settings of the clip it moves, over the tracks of them that still exist, and falls back to the defaults over the other unmuted video and audio tracks for a clip ducked before (or whose tracks are gone); `duck.parameters` in its result says `stored` or `defaults`. `audio.duck` is a schema change (`ClipAudioSchema`, JSON Schema regenerated): an older Frameshell rejects such a timeline.
- **Not done.** The re-run does not re-apply `level` (a move does not change a file's loudness; run `duck --level` again after the voice changes). Candidates are measured over whole files; loudness over the played range and a style default for `--level` (`sound.musicUnderVoice`) are later.

## Slice 4 as built (`music.shape`: step down before a hit, ending)

- **Method.** `music.shape { clip, drop?, ending?, lead?, depth?, hold?, release?, fade?, clear?, dryRun? }` (CLI `music shape <clip> --drop <time>… --ending <time>`, MCP `music_shape`; protocol 120). `drop` is a list of timeline times of hits of the picture (a title slam, a sound effect, the cut into the end card), `ending` the time the closing fade begins; both go through the shared resolver (seconds, a marker id or label, `beat:N` / `bar:N`, an offset). Planned by `music/shape-run.ts`, the curve in `music/shape.ts` (pure), written by `daemon.ts` as one `music.shape` operation in one transaction `Music shape: …` (one proposal in review mode). It is a separate method from `music.fit`: what the music does around a hit does not depend on where the final hit of the file lands, and it is useful on a clip that was never fitted.
- **"Steps down before a hit instead of ducking after it."** For each hit `d` the clip is `depth` dB under its level (default 9) over a quick step (`SHAPE_STEP_S`, 60 ms) that is complete `lead` seconds before `d` (0.4), stays down until `hold` seconds after it (0.3) and comes back over `release` (0.6). That is the duck's shape with its attack moved in front of the event, from a time given instead of one measured on speech. The ending is a straight fade in dB from `ending` over `fade` seconds (2.5) to 60 dB down (inaudible, still a real gain key), held to the clip's end; `--ending bar:N` with a short `--fade` stops the music on a bar line. Where two shapes overlap the deeper one wins at each breakpoint. A hit that the dip never reaches, or an ending after the clip's end, is left out with a warning; nothing left to write is `InvalidOperation`. "Silence, then one soft note" stays a skill technique (decision 6).
- **Keys.** The operation writes `gain` keys marked `auto: "music"` on the clip's own clock, taken from the clip's level (`audio.gain` or its hand keys) with the same lane builder `track.duck` uses (`duckLane` in the engine, now with the mark as a parameter): keys at the clip's start and end, at every curve point inside it and at hand keys. A re-run removes keys with its mark and keeps the others; `clear` removes them. A hand key inside a dip is not dipped (a shaping only adds keys) and stays as set; a warning names it. `clip.key` on a music key clears its mark, as for a duck key. The settings (`drops`, `ending` as resolved timeline seconds, `lead`, `depth`, `hold`, `release`, `fade`) are kept in the clip's `audio.shape`, like `audio.duck`, and removed with the keys.
- **Both dips hold (duck).** The two marks share one lane. `track.duck` treats a music key as part of the level it dips from, like a hand key: a music key inside a dip comes back as a duck key with the level in `base` and `baseAuto: "music"`, and the next duck run gives it its mark back (`restoredKeys` in the engine). `music.shape` removes duck keys when it writes (their values were taken from the old level), so the daemon runs the duck again in the same transaction, with the settings stored on the clip (else the defaults, as for `music.fit`; `duck.parameters` says which). With no track to listen to it warns that the duck keys are gone.
- **`music.fit` runs the shaping again.** The hits are on the picture; the keys are on the clip's clock, so a fit that moves or retimes the clip would shift them (the same failure decision 5 prevents for duck). When the clip carries music keys and the fit changes it, `music.shape` runs again in the same transaction with the stored settings and the clip's new length, after the clip operations and before the duck; `steps` has a `shape` step and `skip: ["shape"]` leaves the keys with a warning. Keys with no stored settings are named in a warning. When the clip also carries duck keys the shaping needs the duck to run again (it removes them): if the duck is skipped or has no track to listen to, the shaping is not run again either, and the warning says so.
- **Schema.** `auto` takes `music`; a key takes `baseAuto: "music"`; clips take `audio.shape` (JSON Schema regenerated). An older Frameshell rejects such a timeline.
- **Not done.** The 5 ms `fadeIn` on every music entry waited for sample-accurate audio fades (#585); built after, below. The first duck's stored parameters (decision 5) were built in slice 3. `duck --level` defaulting from the style's `sound.musicUnderVoice` is not built: it would change what every `duck` does in a project with a style file, and needs an opt-out; left for a decision. The ending does not yet snap itself to a bar line (give `--ending bar:N`). The rising high-pass build (slice 6) and grid moves (slice 5) are later.

## The 5 ms `fadeIn` as built (#485, after #585)

#585 gave media clips `audio.fadeInMs` and `audio.fadeOutMs`: equal-power ramps in milliseconds, sample-accurate, not snapped to frames (ADR 0018 amendment), written by `clip.add` and `clip.set` (`audioFadeInMs`). That is the way to write what decision text lists as "a 5 ms `fadeIn` on every entry"; no new field or operation.

- **Who writes it.** `music.fit` (the `fade` step), `music.shape` (the `fade` step, before the keys) and `music.candidates` (`audioFadeInMs` on each `clip.add`) give the music clip `audio.fadeInMs = 5` (`MUSIC_ENTRY_FADE_MS`, `music/entry-fade.ts`). One transaction as before: the fade is one more `clip.set` in it, so `revert` undoes the fit or shaping whole, fade included, and in review mode it is part of the one proposal.
- **A hand-set fade is kept.** `needsEntryFade` is false when the clip has `audio.fadeInMs`, a frame `fadeIn` or a `dissolve`: the person's (or an earlier run's) edge stays, and a second fit or shape writes nothing more. `skip: ["fade"]` on `music.fit` leaves it out (`music.shape` has no `skip`: to drop the fade, `clip set --audio-fade-in 0` afterwards; the next shape adds it back). `music.shape --clear` and a failed or no-op call leave the clip's edges alone.
- **Where the fit already lands**, the fade is all there is to write: the call is applied (one `clip.set`) and the warning says only the fade in is written, instead of "nothing to change". A fit that does change the clip does not trigger the duck or shape re-run for the fade alone; those still follow the clip moves only.
- **Not the tail.** Decision 6 names no fixed fade out: the ending is the gain-key fade of `music.shape` (2 to 3 s down to inaudible, or a short one on a bar line), so nothing writes `audio.fadeOutMs`. A tail click guard on a clip that ends mid-music is left to the export's 2 ms edge fade.
- **Compatibility.** `audio.fadeInMs` is already in the schema (#585), so no new field: an older Frameshell that has it plays the timelines; one that does not rejects them like any timeline with that field. Wire: `fade` joins the step ids of `music.fit` and `music.shape` (protocol 123).
- **Cut by a later edit.** `clip split` or `cut` drop `audio.fadeInMs` at a new head edge (ADR 0018 amendment), so a music clip cut in two has the fade on the first piece only; a second `music fit` or `music shape` on the new clip gives it one.

## Considered options

- **B. Tape-resample flag in v1** (deferred, decision 2): a new schema field and a second speed semantics for a pitch change of about 0.29 semitone at 1.7 %; added only if the listening check needs it.
- **C. Agent playbook plus a read-only `music.analyze`** (rejected): several transactions per fit, the arithmetic and the 1.7 % refusal redone by every agent, no single `revert`.
- **D. Candidates as a "variant" concept** (rejected): touches schema, engine invariants, patches, review keys, preview, export and the app for what muted tracks already do.

## Consequences

- An older Frameshell cannot read the `auto: "music"` mark, `baseAuto` or `audio.shape` (slice 4) or the swept-filter effect (slice 6); slices 1 and 2 add no schema field, so their timelines open in older versions that understand `speed`, `fadeIn` and keys. Slice 3 adds `audio.duck` to clips (JSON Schema regenerated); a timeline with it is rejected by an older version.
- Until #499, a varisped clip sounds up to about 0.29 semitone different in the preview than in the export, with equal length and timing.
- Candidate tracks add muted tracks; export already skips them. The person deletes the losers until a later `music.pick`.
- A loudness cache under `.frameshell/` (regenerable, swept by `gc`).
- The beat grid file `beats/<stem>.beats.json` is defined once, in ADR 0029 slice 2, with a `detector` field that admits `exact`, before grid moves (slice 5) or sound music (#463) read it (cross rule 3 on #451).
