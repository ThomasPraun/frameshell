# Effects on clips: voice chain, backdrop blur, colour grade

Epic #451 asks for three effects that SPEC §8.2 left as "Effects / filters, v0.3": a **voice chain** on clip sound (#464), a **backdrop blur** under generated layers for glass cards (#462), and a **colour grade** with LUTs (#384). No clip carried any effect before. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-10 (option A of the draft in #462, every recommendation accepted). SPEC decision 36. Slice 1 (audio effects, #464) shipped with this ADR, slice 2 (backdrop, #462) after it, then the grade (#384) after its WebGL2 spike.

## Context (verified in the tree)

- **Clip fields:** `clipBase` in `packages/schema/src/timeline.ts` carries `transform`, `audio { gain, muted }`, transitions and keys for media, timeline and adapter clips. ADR 0018 set the rule this follows: optional flat fields, one evaluator shared by export and preview, files without them unchanged.
- **Export:** `videoGraph` in `packages/core/src/export/compiler.ts` builds a chain per piece and overlays it on what is below. `audioGraph` trims, speeds, gains and fades each item and mixes the tracks. The mix goes to a lossless `mix.wv` before two-pass `loudnorm`, because a large audio graph truncated output on ffmpeg 9.0.2 (ADR 0005).
- **Preview:** the engine worker draws layers on a 2D `OffscreenCanvas`. Sound is plain TypeScript over 48 kHz s16 mono PCM sidecars (`SIDECAR_SAMPLE_RATE`, `SIDECAR_CHANNELS` in `media/recipe.ts`; `preview/audio-plan.ts`), fetched through `frameshell-media://`.
- **Binaries:** the pinned ffmpeg 9.0.2 has every filter needed (`highpass`, `afftdn`, `acompressor`, `asoftclip`, `gblur`, `colorchannelmixer`, `alphamerge`, `lut3d`, `signalstats`, …; checked on darwin-arm64 only). No new binary, model or library.
- **`afftdn` delays its output** by 1200 samples at 48 kHz (its 25 ms window), keeping the input's length: measured on the pinned build.

## Decision

Option A: **typed, flat, optional effect fields per kind** (`audio.effects`, `backdrop`, `grade`), one evaluator each. Timelines without them stay byte-identical. Sound goes through **one baked file** that preview and export both read; colour through **one generated 3D table** read by `lut3d` and the preview; the backdrop is compositor work, so clip render caches are untouched.

1. **Ship order:** audio (#464), then backdrop (#462), then grade (#384). Audio first: it needs no preview engine change.
2. **Backdrop mask:** the clip's alpha above a small threshold in v1. An exact second mask render from the adapter would double HyperFrames render time.
3. **Blur strength:** Gaussian sigma in project pixels, as ffmpeg `gblur` and CSS `blur()` take it.
4. **Grade per clip only** in v1; no track-level grade.
5. **Multicam:** no effects on shots or angles in v1. Like transitions on program tracks, they are skipped with a warning.
6. **Voice chain:** `clip set --voice` copies the voice chain defaults from `style.json` (ADR 0028) into the clip as explicit, editable list entries. The style is where the default comes from, the clip holds its own copy, and an explicit clip effect wins. Integrated loudness stays `export.loudness`.
7. **Soft saturation** (`asoftclip`, tanh drive) is its own effect kind, not part of the voice chain.
8. **LUTs:** the person's `.cube` files plus a few looks generated by Frameshell; no third-party LUT files bundled.
9. **LOG footage** (not Rec.709): warn, never refuse.
10. **Grain, vignette, letterbox:** a separate ticket.

Cross decisions, shared with ADR 0030 (transition shapes) and the matte ADR (#461):

- **Compositor order per part**, the same in preview (`#composite`) and export (`videoGraph`): grade → transition shape → alpha or matte mask → backdrop, which reads what is below.
- **Rewrite order of the timeline before compositing:** `resolveFormat` → `withTrackMutes` → subject layers (#461) → `flattenTimeline`.
- **Preview pitch at speed ≠ 1** is its own ticket (#499); baked audio and voiceover depend on it. As built: the preview stretches a sped-up span's sound with WSOLA (`preview/time-stretch.ts`, called by `renderRead`): Hann frames of 2048 samples (export `atempo`'s window at 48 kHz) every 1024 program samples, each centred where the speed puts it and moved by up to 512 samples to line up with the frame before (normalized correlation less a small cost per sample moved, so a held note does not sit early or late; silence keeps the frame in place). Source outside the part the clip plays is silence to the stretch, as export trims before `atempo`, so a cut word never bleeds in. The alignment is a chain the engine worker carries from one read of a span to the next during a play (`StretchChain`, reads of one span rendered in order); a seek starts a new one. Speed 1 is unchanged. Export keeps `atempo` with no pre-roll (owner, 2026-11-13; numbers and the 21 ms limit after digital silence in ADR 0037). `preview-pitch-parity.test.ts` checks tones at 0.75, 1.25 and 2 against the export mix: pitch within 2 Hz, level on time within 128 samples, fades alike.
- Each side of a dissolve on the bottom track is graded before the mix (golden test).
- Energy snapping and transcription keep reading the **dry** sidecar or asset, by design.

Rules from the WebGL2 spike G0 (`docs/research/webgl2-grade.md`, PR #518; owner, 2026-11-13):

- **The preview samples the 3D table the way ffmpeg `lut3d` does:** 8 taps of a 32-bit float table, mixed red, then green, then blue, in single precision, and the result truncated to 8 bit, as `lut3d=interp=trilinear` does (it truncates, it does not round). Hardware filtering of the table is not used: it rounds and is off by 1 on about 2 % of channels.
- **The colour conversion stays as it is on both sides:** export keeps ffmpeg's automatic YUV to RGB conversion (no `in_color_matrix`, `full_chroma_int` or `accurate_rnd` added for graded clips) and the preview keeps Chromium's. Parity is defined after that conversion; the chroma-upsampling difference between them, which the ungraded preview already has, is accepted. Changing one side needs the proxy recipe changed in the same step (`RECIPE_VERSION`).

### Slice 1: audio effects (#464)

- **Schema:** media clips take `audio.effects`, a list (1 to 8) applied in order. Kinds: `highpass { frequency }` (Hz), `denoise { floor, reduction }` (dB), `compressor { threshold, ratio, attack, release, makeup }` (dB, ratio, ms, ms, dB). An omitted number takes the voice chain's, which is every parameter's default. Ranges are the pinned ffmpeg's, in dB and ms (`AUDIO_EFFECT_LIMITS`). Only media clips: a nested timeline host's chain over its clips would need a second bake, and generated clips have no sound. `packages/schema/src/effects.ts` holds the normalization (`normalizeAudioEffects`, `audioEffectsKey`, `bakeRef`) without Zod, because the preview worker imports it; the Zod schema is in `timeline.ts`.
- **Voice chain numbers** (`VOICE_CHAIN`): high-pass 85 Hz; `afftdn` noise floor −30 dB, reduction 8 dB; compressor −20 dB, 2.5:1, attack 8 ms, release 140 ms, makeup +2 dB. These are also the default of the style's `sound.voice` (ADR 0028; its high-pass default is 85 Hz so both agree, pinned by a test). `clip set --voice` reads the style (`style.show`) in the CLI and writes `voiceChainOf(sound.voice)` (`packages/schema/src/style.ts`) as explicit entries: seconds to ms, each number held to `AUDIO_EFFECT_LIMITS`, and a noise reduction of 0 leaves `denoise` out. A project without `style.json` gets the numbers above. The clip's entries stay its own: a later style change does not touch clips already set. No wire change; MCP clients read `style_show` and pass `audioEffects`.
- **Bake** (`packages/core/src/media/audio-fx.ts`): the asset's first audio stream, put on the sidecar clock (`alignedAudioFilter`), downmixed to mono, then `highpass` / `afftdn` / `acompressor` in list order, written as raw s16le at the sidecar's rate and channels to `.frameshell/audio-fx/<key>.pcm`. Key = 20 hex digits of the content hash, 12 of the SHA-256 of the normalized chain, and `AUDIO_FX_VERSION`. Each `denoise` adds 1200 samples of padding at the end and trims them from the start (`DENOISE_LATENCY_SAMPLES`, pinned by a test), so the bake has the sidecar's length and timing. One bake per asset content and chain, shared by every clip that plays it. A bake processes the whole asset: the filter state at a clip's `in` is the continuous file's, not reset at the clip.
- **When it bakes:** the preview asks `audio.bakes` (internal method) on every revision; it queues missing bakes as `audio-fx` jobs and reports each as `ready`, `queued`, `baking` or `failed`. Export bakes what it still needs before the mix (render step `audio-fx`). Bakes run outside the queue's slots once started, so a render waiting on one cannot starve the queue.
- **Export:** `audioGraph` reads a clip's bake instead of its asset (`RenderInput.audioFx` by `bakeRef`), as raw PCM input, spread to both channels at full level (`pan=stereo|c0=c0|c1=c0`). Trim, speed, gain, keys, fades and loudness work as before. A clip whose bake is missing plays dry, with a warning. Plans of timelines without effects are unchanged; the existing goldens did not change and a new one (`audio-fx-plan`) covers the bake inputs.
- **Preview:** `compileProgram` takes the ready bakes (`ProgramOptions.bakes`) and plays a clip's bake as its sidecar, the dry sidecar until it is ready. The engine is unchanged; `frameshell-media://` also serves `.frameshell/audio-fx/*.pcm`. A test checks that the preview's samples and the export mix of the same clip are equal within 1e-6.
- **Protocol 83:** `clip.set` takes `audioEffects` (a list replaces the clip's; `null` clears); a shot gets a warning and keeps its fields. Job kind and render step `audio-fx`, `gc` entry kind and skipped area `audio-fx`, internal method `audio.bakes`. MCP `clip_set` follows from the registry.
- **CLI:** `frameshell clip set <clip> --voice | --audio-effects '<json list>' | --clear-audio-effects`.
- **`gc`:** sweeps bakes no clip of any timeline plays now, and temps of interrupted bakes; nothing keyed while an export runs or a timeline cannot be read.
- `RECIPE_VERSION` and `CACHE_LAYOUT_VERSION` do not change.

### Slice 2: backdrop (#462)

- **Schema:** adapter (generated) clips take `backdrop { blur, saturate?, dim? }`: `blur` is the Gaussian sigma in project pixels (0 to 200), `saturate` the CSS `saturate()` factor (0 to 4, default 1), `dim` the alpha of a black scrim over the blurred picture (0 to 1, default 0). Kit glass (`GLASS`): 46, 1.45, 0.55; 0.72 over bright footage. Media and nested timeline clips take none: the glass belongs to the card, and footage has no alpha to mask it by. `packages/schema/src/effects.ts` holds the one layer description without Zod (`backdropOf`, `backdropLayer`, `backdropMatrix`, `BACKDROP_ALPHA_THRESHOLD`); export and the preview engine both read it.
- **Layer description** (`backdropLayer`): sigma = `blur` times the output's short side over the project's (a 960x540 canvas of a 1080p project blurs at 23; a 9:16 format of a 16:9 project keeps 46), `saturate`, `dim`, and the mask threshold 8 of 255 (about 3 %; decision 2). The colour step is one matrix (`backdropMatrix`): the Filter Effects saturate matrix on gamma-encoded RGB, times `1 - dim` (black at `dim` alpha over it).
- **Where it draws:** upper video tracks only. The bottom picture track has nothing below it: `clip.set` stores the backdrop with a warning, export draws none and warns, the preview draws none. Shots take none (decision 5).
- **Export** (`backdropUnder` in `compiler.ts`): the piece's chain splits after its picture is scaled to rgba, before opacity. One branch is the piece as before. The other cuts its alpha at the threshold (`lutrgb=a='gt(val,8)*255'`), then takes the same opacity, keyed opacity, fades, wipe mask and timing, so the glass fades and wipes with its card; it is overlaid at the piece's own position on a transparent frame and its alpha extracted (`alphaextract`). What is below is split: one copy goes through `format=gbrp,gblur=sigma=…:steps=3,colorchannelmixer=…,format=rgba`, takes that mask (`alphamerge`) and is overlaid on the other, then the piece is overlaid as before. `steps=3`: the default single pass has the right sigma but peaks 1.77x a Gaussian (3 passes: 1.15x, near the canvas `blur()`); a render test checks an impulse row's sigma, peak and tail beyond 3 sigma. Blur and matrix carry `enable='between(n,…)'` when the piece covers only part of the segment. A zero blur or an identity matrix leaves its filter out. A timeline without backdrops compiles byte for byte as before (every existing golden unchanged); `backdrop-plan` covers the new graph. A render test with the managed ffmpeg checks that under a 15 % alpha pane 2 px stripes come out flat at the dimmed level and stay sharp beside it and before the clip.
- **Preview** (`preview/backdrop.ts`, `BackdropPass`, called from `#composite` in the engine worker): before the picture, the canvas so far is drawn into a scratch canvas through `filter = blur(σ) saturate(s)` (`canvasBackdrop`), from a copy padded 3 sigma out with its edge pixels repeated, dimmed with black at `dim` (`source-atop`, keeping the blur's edge alpha), cut by `destination-in` with a mask made from the render's alpha plane at the same threshold, drawn at the picture's rectangle with its opacity, and drawn back. A shaped dissolve's clip region limits it as it limits the picture. `program.ts` carries the backdrop on the generated span (`glass`) of upper tracks only. A parity test compares the preview's span and canvas steps with export's graph for the same timeline at two output sizes (sigma, matrix, threshold, bottom track).
- **Order per part** (decisions above): alpha and matte, then the backdrop, which reads everything drawn below it, subject layers and shaped pieces included.
- **Cost** (Electron 44 worker `OffscreenCanvas`, Apple M3, one glass card covering a quarter of the frame, 60 frames): a composite of picture and card took 1.1 ms median at 1920x1080 and 4.8 ms with the backdrop (p95 1.8 and 6.4 ms); at 960x540, 0.7 and 2.4 ms (p95 2.4 and 3.3 ms). About 4 ms per glass layer at full HD canvas size, inside one 60 Hz frame (16.7 ms) and the ADR 0001 freeze p95 limit (17 ms). The ADR 0001 thresholds harness was not rerun.
- **Style:** `style.json` `glass { cards, blur, saturate, dim }` (ADR 0028). `cards` defaults to false: glass is a style choice, never a default (HyperFrames lists glass cards everywhere among AI-looking defaults). The numbers default to the kit's. `clip set --glass` reads the style (`style.show`) and writes `glassOf(glass)` as the clip's explicit `backdrop`; `--backdrop-blur`, `--backdrop-saturate`, `--backdrop-dim` set one number each, over `--glass`; `--clear-backdrop` removes it. The clip keeps its copy when the style changes later.
- **Protocol 102:** `clip.set` takes `backdrop` (fields merged into the clip's, `blur` needed once, `null` clears); `style.show` gains `glass`. MCP `clip_set` follows from the registry.
- **App:** the clip inspector shows a Glass switch for generated clips on video tracks: on, it writes the style's glass numbers (read from `style.json`); then Blur, Saturate and Dim fields. Strings in `shared/i18n/inspector.ts` (en + es).
- **Limits:** the mask is the render's alpha above the threshold, so soft shadows above about 3 % get glass too. The canvas blur treats outside the frame as transparent, so the preview blurs a copy of the canvas whose edge pixels are stretched 3 sigma out, as `gblur` repeats the edge. Live preview pages (ADR 0010) show no backdrop while a generated clip's page plays instead of its render. `CACHE_LAYOUT_VERSION` and `RECIPE_VERSION` do not change: renders are untouched.

### Slice 3: Voice section of the clip inspector (#464)

- **What it shows:** media clips of a video project get a Voice group in the clip inspector (`components/VoiceControl.tsx`). The Voice switch is on while the clip has effects. Switching on writes the style's voice chain (`voiceChainOf` over `style.json` `sound.voice`, read in the renderer by `project-style.ts`, shared with the Glass switch; the numbers above without a style); the clip keeps its copy. While on, each effect is a row in processing order: its numbers (`NumberField`, held to `AUDIO_EFFECT_LIMITS`; a number outside the range is refused in the field), a move-earlier button and a remove button. A menu adds a kind the chain lacks, at the end; Voice preset replaces the chain with the style's.
- **Bypass:** the model has no bypass flag (decision 6: the clip holds explicit entries), so the switch off clears `audio.effects` and remembers the chain in this window (`bypass` in `voice/model.ts`); switching on again writes it back instead of the style's. The memory is not saved with the project: another window or a restart gets the style's chain. A per-effect or clip-level `bypass` field would be a model change for its own slice.
- **Writes:** every change is one `clip.set` `audioEffects` by `ui` with the whole list (`null` when it empties), so undo, history and review mode work as for any edit; no wire change. The preview asks for the bake on the next revision (`useAudioBakes`) and plays the dry sound until it is ready.
- **Strings:** `shared/i18n/inspector.ts` (en + es). e2e `voice-inspector.spec.ts`; pure edits in `apps/desktop/test/voice-model.test.ts`.
- **Out:** stereo bakes and effects on the host clip of a nested timeline stay out (they need a second bake).

### Slice 4: colour grade, export and preview (G1 + G2, #384)

- **Schema:** media clips take `grade { exposure, temperature, tint, contrast, saturation, lut, lutMix }` (`GradeSchema`; ranges `GRADE_LIMITS`: stops -4 to 4; -1 to 1; factors 0 to 3; 0 to 1). Media clips only: a nested timeline host would need table composition, and grading a generated clip's alpha render is not measured yet. Audio tracks take none. A grade whose numbers change nothing (and no LUT, or `lutMix` 0) is no grade (`gradeOf`). `packages/schema/src/grade.ts` (no Zod, the engine worker imports it) holds the one evaluator: `parseCube` (Adobe Cube 1.0: `LUT_3D_SIZE` 2 to 65, `DOMAIN_MIN`/`DOMAIN_MAX`, `LUT_3D_INPUT_RANGE`, comments; 1D LUTs refused), `gradeTable` and `applyGradeTable`.
- **The table** (`gradeTable`): at every lattice point, exposure (`2^exposure`) and white balance (gains `1 ± 0.2·temperature` on red and blue, `1 - 0.2·tint` on green) on light (BT.1886 gamma 2.4), then contrast about 0.5 and saturation about Rec.709 luma on the encoded values, each clamped to 0 to 1; then the LUT sampled trilinearly through its domain and mixed in by `lutMix`. 33 points per side, or the LUT's own size when larger. Every value is rounded to 6 decimals, the precision of the `.cube` text export writes (`cubeText`), so the preview uploads the numbers ffmpeg parses. Correct first, then style (#384).
- **Export** (`gradedChain` in `compiler.ts`): a graded piece's chain is `pieceChain`, then `format=rgba,lut3d=file=<table>:interp=trilinear`, before crop, scale, opacity, fades, wipe mask and matte, on the bottom track (both sides of a dissolve, each its own grade) and on upper tracks. `RenderInput.grades`/`FrameInput.grades` name each table file by `gradeKey`; `gradeFiles` (`export/grade.ts`) reads each LUT once, writes nothing itself, and the service writes the `.cube` texts into the render or frame work directory, removed with it (no cache, nothing for `gc`). A LUT outside the project, missing or not a 3D `.cube` fails the request (`ExportUnsupported`, naming the clip); a graded clip without a table is drawn ungraded with a warning. Plans of timelines without grades are unchanged (every existing golden); `grade-plan` covers a graded dissolve. `grade-render.test.ts` runs the compiled frame through the managed ffmpeg against `applyGradeTable` on a random still: 0 of 18,432 channels differ on darwin-arm64.
- **Preview** (`preview/grade.ts`, `GradePass`, called from `#composite`): one WebGL2 `OffscreenCanvas` per engine, made with the first program that has a grade (context and shader take up to 0.4 s cold), not on the first graded frame; a program without grades never makes it. Per graded part: `texImage2D` of the decoded `VideoFrame` or still (no premultiply, flip or colour conversion on upload), one full-screen pass with the 8-tap shader, `transferToImageBitmap`, then the rest of `#composite` as before (alpha mask, placement, cover crop, shape) on that bitmap, closed after the draw. Parts without a grade take the old path: no extra copy. Tables are uploaded as `RGB32F` 3D textures once per `gradeKey` when the program arrives and dropped when no program uses them. The renderer makes the tables (`useGradeTables`: LUTs read through the window, again when the file changes, last 32 tables kept) and `compileProgram` puts the ones in use in `Program.grades`, the span keyed by `grade`; a grade whose LUT is not read yet or cannot be used draws ungraded, with a console warning. `EngineStats.graded` counts passes.
- **Fallback:** no WebGL2, a shader that does not compile, or a lost context: the part is drawn ungraded and the engine says so once (`gradeOff`); the preview shows a notice (`shared/i18n/preview.ts`, en + es). A restored context is rebuilt on the next frame.
- **Parity, measured:** `grade-preview.spec.ts` grades a 960x540 random still with a strong correction and a 17-point LUT and compares the preview canvas with `frameshell frame`: the test allows 1 level on under 0.1 % of channels; measured on darwin-arm64 (Apple M3, ANGLE Metal), no channel differs. `preview-grade.test.ts` checks the preview uploads the same table export writes, for the same clips. Video adds the chroma-upsampling difference of the second rule above.
- **Protocol 127:** `clip.set` takes `grade` (merged field by field; `lut: null` removes the LUT, `null` clears the grade); shots skip it with a warning. MCP `clip_set` follows from the registry. **CLI:** `clip set --grade-exposure`, `--grade-temperature`, `--grade-tint`, `--grade-contrast`, `--grade-saturation`, `--grade-lut <file | none>`, `--grade-lut-mix`, `--clear-grade`.
- `RECIPE_VERSION` and `CACHE_LAYOUT_VERSION` do not change; ADR 0001's thresholds are unchanged and its harness was not rerun with graded clips.
- **Not yet:** the clip inspector's Colour section, LOG footage warning (decision 9), looks generated by Frameshell (decision 8), `grade.analyze` and `grade.compare`, and grades on stills with alpha checked against export (the pass keeps straight alpha; not measured).

## Consequences

- An effect change takes seconds to bake before the preview hears it; the dry sound plays meanwhile. Export always waits for the bake.
- The bake is mono, like the sidecar, so a stereo clip with effects plays as mono in export. Voice, which the chain is for, is mono.
- Compression and denoise change the mix before `loudnorm`; the final level is still `export.loudness`. `transcribe --verify` of an export hears the processed sound.
- Long chains stay out of the big mix graph that ADR 0005 found fragile.
- The effect kinds are a closed set: a new kind needs core code. Plugin effects (SPEC §8.2, v0.3) can map onto these kinds later without a model break.
- Disk: one 48 kHz mono s16 file per asset and chain, about 165 MiB per 30 minutes, regenerable.
