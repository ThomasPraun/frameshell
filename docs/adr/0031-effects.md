# Effects on clips: voice chain, backdrop blur, colour grade

Epic #451 asks for three effects that SPEC §8.2 left as "Effects / filters, v0.3": a **voice chain** on clip sound (#464), a **backdrop blur** under generated layers for glass cards (#462), and a **colour grade** with LUTs (#384). No clip carried any effect before. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-10 (option A of the draft in #462, every recommendation accepted). SPEC decision 36. Slice 1 (audio effects, #464) shipped with this ADR, slice 2 (backdrop, #462) after it, then the grade (#384) after its WebGL2 spike.

## Context (verified in the tree)

- **Clip fields:** `clipBase` in `packages/schema/src/timeline.ts` carries `transform`, `audio { gain, muted }`, transitions and keys for media, timeline and adapter clips. ADR 0018 set the rule this follows: optional flat fields, one evaluator shared by export and preview, files without them unchanged.
- **Export:** `videoGraph` in `packages/core/src/export/compiler.ts` builds a chain per piece and overlays it on what is below. `audioGraph` trims, speeds, gains and fades each item and mixes the tracks. The mix goes to a lossless `mix.wv` before two-pass `loudnorm`, because a large audio graph truncated output on ffmpeg 9.0.2 (ADR 0005).
- **Preview:** the engine worker draws layers on a 2D `OffscreenCanvas`. Sound is plain TypeScript over 48 kHz s16 mono PCM sidecars (`SIDECAR_SAMPLE_RATE`, `SIDECAR_CHANNELS` in `media/recipe.ts`; `preview/audio-plan.ts`), fetched through `frameshell-media://`.
- **Binaries:** the pinned ffmpeg 9.0.2 has every filter needed (`highpass`, `afftdn`, `acompressor`, `asoftclip`, `gblur`, `colorchannelmixer`, `alphamerge`, `lut3d`, `signalstats`, …; checked on darwin-arm64 only). No new binary, model or library.
- **`afftdn` delays its output** by 1200 samples at 48 kHz (its 25 ms window), keeping the input's length: measured on the pinned build.

## Decision

Option A: **typed, flat, optional effect fields per kind** (`audio.effects`, `backdrop`, `grade`), one evaluator each. Timelines without them stay byte-identical. Sound goes through **one baked file** that preview and export both read; colour through **one generated 3D table** read by `lut3d` and the preview; the backdrop is compositor work, so clip render caches are untouched.

1. **Ship order:** audio (#464), then backdrop (#462), then grade (#384). Audio first: it needs no preview engine change.
2. **Backdrop mask:** the clip's alpha above a small threshold in v1. An exact second mask render from the adapter would double HyperFrames render time.
3. **Blur strength:** Gaussian sigma in project pixels, as ffmpeg `gblur` and CSS `blur()` take it.
4. **Grade per clip only** in v1; no track-level grade.
5. **Multicam:** no effects on shots or angles in v1. Like transitions on program tracks, they are skipped with a warning.
6. **Voice chain:** `clip set --voice` copies the voice chain defaults from `style.json` (ADR 0028) into the clip as explicit, editable list entries. The style is where the default comes from, the clip holds its own copy, and an explicit clip effect wins. Integrated loudness stays `export.loudness`.
7. **Soft saturation** (`asoftclip`, tanh drive) is its own effect kind, not part of the voice chain.
8. **LUTs:** the person's `.cube` files plus a few built-in looks; no third-party LUT files bundled. The built-in looks are plain grade numbers in one table, not generated `.cube` files (slice 10).
9. **LOG footage** (not Rec.709): warn, never refuse.
10. **Grain, vignette, letterbox:** a separate ticket.

Cross decisions, shared with ADR 0030 (transition shapes) and the matte ADR (#461):

- **Compositor order per part**, the same in preview (`#composite`) and export (`videoGraph`): grade → transition shape → alpha or matte mask → backdrop, which reads what is below. Amended by ADR 0043: grade → vignette → grain → transition shape → alpha or matte mask → backdrop; the timeline's letterbox comes after every part, before the captions.
- **Rewrite order of the timeline before compositing:** `resolveFormat` → `withTrackMutes` → subject layers (#461) → `flattenTimeline`.
- **Preview pitch at speed ≠ 1** is its own ticket (#499); baked audio and voiceover depend on it. As built: the preview stretches a sped-up span's sound with WSOLA (`preview/time-stretch.ts`, called by `renderRead`): Hann frames of 2048 samples (export `atempo`'s window at 48 kHz) every 1024 program samples, each centred where the speed puts it and moved by up to 512 samples to line up with the frame before (normalized correlation less a small cost per sample moved, so a held note does not sit early or late; silence keeps the frame in place). Source outside the part the clip plays is silence to the stretch, as export trims before `atempo`, so a cut word never bleeds in. The alignment is a chain the engine worker carries from one read of a span to the next during a play (`StretchChain`, reads of one span rendered in order); a seek starts a new one. Speed 1 is unchanged. Export keeps `atempo` with no pre-roll (owner, 2026-11-13; numbers and the 21 ms limit after digital silence in ADR 0037). `preview-pitch-parity.test.ts` checks tones at 0.75, 1.25 and 2 against the export mix: pitch within 2 Hz, level on time within 128 samples, fades alike.
- Each side of a dissolve on the bottom track is graded before the mix (golden test).
- Energy snapping and transcription keep reading the **dry** sidecar or asset, by design.

Rules from the WebGL2 spike G0 (`docs/research/webgl2-grade.md`, PR #518; owner, 2026-11-13):

- **The preview samples the 3D table the way ffmpeg `lut3d` does:** 8 taps of a 32-bit float table, mixed red, then green, then blue, in single precision, and the result truncated to 8 bit, as `lut3d=interp=trilinear` does (it truncates, it does not round). Hardware filtering of the table is not used: it rounds and is off by 1 on about 2 % of channels.
- **The colour conversion stays as it is on both sides:** export keeps ffmpeg's automatic YUV to RGB conversion (no `in_color_matrix`, `full_chroma_int` or `accurate_rnd` added for graded clips) and the preview keeps Chromium's. Parity is defined after that conversion; the chroma-upsampling difference between them, which the ungraded preview already has, is accepted. Changing one side needs the proxy recipe changed in the same step (`RECIPE_VERSION`).

### Slice 1: audio effects (#464)

- **Schema:** media clips take `audio.effects`, a list (1 to 8) applied in order. Kinds: `highpass { frequency }` (Hz), `denoise { floor, reduction }` (dB), `compressor { threshold, ratio, attack, release, makeup }` (dB, ratio, ms, ms, dB); later `highpassSweep { from, to, startHz, endHz }` (ADR 0037 slice 6: a high-pass whose cutoff rises over a window of the asset). An omitted number takes the voice chain's, which is every parameter's default. Ranges are the pinned ffmpeg's, in dB and ms (`AUDIO_EFFECT_LIMITS`). Only media clips: a nested timeline host's chain over its clips would need a second bake, and generated clips have no sound. `packages/schema/src/effects.ts` holds the normalization (`normalizeAudioEffects`, `audioEffectsKey`, `bakeRef`) without Zod, because the preview worker imports it; the Zod schema is in `timeline.ts`.
- **Voice chain numbers** (`VOICE_CHAIN`): high-pass 85 Hz; `afftdn` noise floor −30 dB, reduction 8 dB; compressor −20 dB, 2.5:1, attack 8 ms, release 140 ms, makeup +2 dB. These are also the default of the style's `sound.voice` (ADR 0028; its high-pass default is 85 Hz so both agree, pinned by a test). `clip set --voice` reads the style (`style.show`) in the CLI and writes `voiceChainOf(sound.voice)` (`packages/schema/src/style.ts`) as explicit entries: seconds to ms, each number held to `AUDIO_EFFECT_LIMITS`, and a noise reduction of 0 leaves `denoise` out. A project without `style.json` gets the numbers above. The clip's entries stay its own: a later style change does not touch clips already set. No wire change; MCP clients read `style_show` and pass `audioEffects`.
- **Bake** (`packages/core/src/media/audio-fx.ts`): the asset's first audio stream, put on the sidecar clock (`alignedAudioFilter`), downmixed to mono, then `highpass` / `afftdn` / `acompressor` in list order, written as raw s16le at the sidecar's rate and channels to `.frameshell/audio-fx/<key>.pcm`. Key = 20 hex digits of the content hash, 12 of the SHA-256 of the normalized chain, and `AUDIO_FX_VERSION`. Each `denoise` adds 1200 samples of padding at the end and trims them from the start (`DENOISE_LATENCY_SAMPLES`, pinned by a test), so the bake has the sidecar's length and timing. One bake per asset content and chain, shared by every clip that plays it. A bake processes the whole asset: the filter state at a clip's `in` is the continuous file's, not reset at the clip.
- **When it bakes:** the preview asks `audio.bakes` (internal method) on every revision; it queues missing bakes as `audio-fx` jobs and reports each as `ready`, `queued`, `baking` or `failed`. Export bakes what it still needs before the mix (render step `audio-fx`). Bakes run outside the queue's slots once started, so a render waiting on one cannot starve the queue.
- **Export:** `audioGraph` reads a clip's bake instead of its asset (`RenderInput.audioFx` by `bakeRef`), as raw PCM input, spread to both channels at full level (`pan=stereo|c0=c0|c1=c0`). Trim, speed, gain, keys, fades and loudness work as before. A clip whose bake is missing plays dry, with a warning. Plans of timelines without effects are unchanged; the existing goldens did not change and a new one (`audio-fx-plan`) covers the bake inputs.
- **Preview:** `compileProgram` takes the ready bakes (`ProgramOptions.bakes`) and plays a clip's bake as its sidecar, the dry sidecar until it is ready. The engine is unchanged; `frameshell-media://` also serves `.frameshell/audio-fx/*.pcm`. A test checks that the preview's samples and the export mix of the same clip are equal within 1e-6.
- **Protocol 83:** `clip.set` takes `audioEffects` (a list replaces the clip's; `null` clears); a shot gets a warning and keeps its fields. Job kind and render step `audio-fx`, `gc` entry kind and skipped area `audio-fx`, internal method `audio.bakes`. MCP `clip_set` follows from the registry.
- **CLI:** `frameshell clip set <clip> --voice | --audio-effects '<json list>' | --clear-audio-effects`.
- **`gc`:** sweeps bakes no clip of any timeline plays now, and temps of interrupted bakes; nothing keyed while an export runs or a timeline cannot be read.
- `RECIPE_VERSION` and `CACHE_LAYOUT_VERSION` do not change.

### Slice 2: backdrop (#462)

- **Schema:** adapter (generated) clips take `backdrop { blur, saturate?, dim? }`: `blur` is the Gaussian sigma in project pixels (0 to 200), `saturate` the CSS `saturate()` factor (0 to 4, default 1), `dim` the alpha of a black scrim over the blurred picture (0 to 1, default 0). Kit glass (`GLASS`): 46, 1.45, 0.55; 0.72 over bright footage. Media and nested timeline clips take none: the glass belongs to the card, and footage has no alpha to mask it by. `packages/schema/src/effects.ts` holds the one layer description without Zod (`backdropOf`, `backdropLayer`, `backdropMatrix`, `BACKDROP_ALPHA_THRESHOLD`); export and the preview engine both read it.
- **Layer description** (`backdropLayer`): sigma = `blur` times the output's short side over the project's (a 960x540 canvas of a 1080p project blurs at 23; a 9:16 format of a 16:9 project keeps 46), `saturate`, `dim`, and the mask threshold 8 of 255 (about 3 %; decision 2). The colour step is one matrix (`backdropMatrix`): the Filter Effects saturate matrix on gamma-encoded RGB, times `1 - dim` (black at `dim` alpha over it).
- **Where it draws:** upper video tracks only. The bottom picture track has nothing below it: `clip.set` stores the backdrop with a warning, export draws none and warns, the preview draws none. Shots take none (decision 5).
- **Export** (`backdropUnder` in `compiler.ts`): the piece's chain splits after its picture is scaled to rgba, before opacity. One branch is the piece as before. The other cuts its alpha at the threshold (`lutrgb=a='gt(val,8)*255'`), then takes the same opacity, keyed opacity, fades, wipe mask and timing, so the glass fades and wipes with its card; it is overlaid at the piece's own position on a transparent frame and its alpha extracted (`alphaextract`). What is below is split: one copy goes through `format=gbrp,gblur=sigma=…:steps=3,colorchannelmixer=…,format=rgba`, takes that mask (`alphamerge`) and is overlaid on the other, then the piece is overlaid as before. `steps=3`: the default single pass has the right sigma but peaks 1.77x a Gaussian (3 passes: 1.15x, near the canvas `blur()`); a render test checks an impulse row's sigma, peak and tail beyond 3 sigma. Blur and matrix carry `enable='between(n,…)'` when the piece covers only part of the segment. A zero blur or an identity matrix leaves its filter out. A timeline without backdrops compiles byte for byte as before (every existing golden unchanged); `backdrop-plan` covers the new graph. A render test with the managed ffmpeg checks that under a 15 % alpha pane 2 px stripes come out flat at the dimmed level and stay sharp beside it and before the clip.
- **Preview** (`preview/backdrop.ts`, `BackdropPass`, called from `#composite` in the engine worker): before the picture, the canvas so far is drawn into a scratch canvas through `filter = blur(σ) saturate(s)` (`canvasBackdrop`), from a copy padded 3 sigma out with its edge pixels repeated, dimmed with black at `dim` (`source-atop`, keeping the blur's edge alpha), cut by `destination-in` with a mask made from the render's alpha plane at the same threshold, drawn at the picture's rectangle with its opacity, and drawn back. A shaped dissolve's clip region limits it as it limits the picture. `program.ts` carries the backdrop on the generated span (`glass`) of upper tracks only. A parity test compares the preview's span and canvas steps with export's graph for the same timeline at two output sizes (sigma, matrix, threshold, bottom track).
- **Order per part** (decisions above): alpha and matte, then the backdrop, which reads everything drawn below it, subject layers and shaped pieces included.
- **Cost** (Electron 44 worker `OffscreenCanvas`, Apple M3, one glass card covering a quarter of the frame, 60 frames): a composite of picture and card took 1.1 ms median at 1920x1080 and 4.8 ms with the backdrop (p95 1.8 and 6.4 ms); at 960x540, 0.7 and 2.4 ms (p95 2.4 and 3.3 ms). About 4 ms per glass layer at full HD canvas size, inside one 60 Hz frame (16.7 ms) and the ADR 0001 freeze p95 limit (17 ms). The ADR 0001 thresholds harness was not rerun.
- **Style:** `style.json` `glass { cards, blur, saturate, dim }` (ADR 0028). `cards` defaults to false: glass is a style choice, never a default (HyperFrames lists glass cards everywhere among AI-looking defaults). The numbers default to the kit's. `clip set --glass` reads the style (`style.show`) and writes `glassOf(glass)` as the clip's explicit `backdrop`; `--backdrop-blur`, `--backdrop-saturate`, `--backdrop-dim` set one number each, over `--glass`; `--clear-backdrop` removes it. The clip keeps its copy when the style changes later.
- **Protocol 102:** `clip.set` takes `backdrop` (fields merged into the clip's, `blur` needed once, `null` clears); `style.show` gains `glass`. MCP `clip_set` follows from the registry.
- **App:** the clip inspector shows a Glass switch for generated clips on video tracks: on, it writes the style's glass numbers (read from `style.json`); then Blur, Saturate and Dim fields. Strings in `shared/i18n/inspector.ts` (en + es).
- **Limits:** the mask is the render's alpha above the threshold, so soft shadows above about 3 % get glass too. The canvas blur treats outside the frame as transparent, so the preview blurs a copy of the canvas whose edge pixels are stretched 3 sigma out, as `gblur` repeats the edge. Live preview pages (ADR 0010) show no backdrop while a generated clip's page plays instead of its render. `CACHE_LAYOUT_VERSION` and `RECIPE_VERSION` do not change: renders are untouched.

### Slice 3: Voice section of the clip inspector (#464)

- **What it shows:** media clips of a video project get a Voice group in the clip inspector (`components/VoiceControl.tsx`). The Voice switch is on while the clip has effects. Switching on writes the style's voice chain (`voiceChainOf` over `style.json` `sound.voice`, read in the renderer by `project-style.ts`, shared with the Glass switch; the numbers above without a style); the clip keeps its copy. While on, each effect is a row in processing order: its numbers (`NumberField`, held to `AUDIO_EFFECT_LIMITS`; a number outside the range is refused in the field), a move-earlier button and a remove button. A menu adds a kind the chain lacks, at the end; Voice preset replaces the chain with the style's.
- **Bypass:** the model has no bypass flag (decision 6: the clip holds explicit entries), so the switch off clears `audio.effects` and remembers the chain in this window (`bypass` in `voice/model.ts`); switching on again writes it back instead of the style's. The memory is not saved with the project: another window or a restart gets the style's chain. A per-effect or clip-level `bypass` field would be a model change for its own slice.
- **Writes:** every change is one `clip.set` `audioEffects` by `ui` with the whole list (`null` when it empties), so undo, history and review mode work as for any edit; no wire change. The preview asks for the bake on the next revision (`useAudioBakes`) and plays the dry sound until it is ready.
- **Strings:** `shared/i18n/inspector.ts` (en + es). e2e `voice-inspector.spec.ts`; pure edits in `apps/desktop/test/voice-model.test.ts`.
- **Out:** stereo bakes and effects on the host clip of a nested timeline stay out (they need a second bake).

### Slice 4: colour grade, export and preview (G1 + G2, #384)

- **Schema:** media clips take `grade { exposure, temperature, tint, contrast, saturation, lut, lutMix }` (`GradeSchema`; ranges `GRADE_LIMITS`: stops -4 to 4; -1 to 1; factors 0 to 3; 0 to 1). Media clips only in this slice: a nested timeline host would need table composition, and grading a generated clip's alpha render is not measured yet (both built in slice 8). Audio tracks take none. A grade whose numbers change nothing (and no LUT, or `lutMix` 0) is no grade (`gradeOf`). `packages/schema/src/grade.ts` (no Zod, the engine worker imports it) holds the one evaluator: `parseCube` (Adobe Cube 1.0: `LUT_3D_SIZE` 2 to 65, `DOMAIN_MIN`/`DOMAIN_MAX`, `LUT_3D_INPUT_RANGE`, comments; 1D LUTs refused), `gradeTable` and `applyGradeTable`.
- **The table** (`gradeTable`): at every lattice point, exposure (`2^exposure`) and white balance (gains `1 ± 0.2·temperature` on red and blue, `1 - 0.2·tint` on green) on light (BT.1886 gamma 2.4), then contrast about 0.5 and saturation about Rec.709 luma on the encoded values, each clamped to 0 to 1; then the LUT sampled trilinearly through its domain and mixed in by `lutMix`. 33 points per side, or the LUT's own size when larger. Every value is rounded to 6 decimals, the precision of the `.cube` text export writes (`cubeText`), so the preview uploads the numbers ffmpeg parses. Correct first, then style (#384).
- **Export** (`gradedChain` in `compiler.ts`): a graded piece's chain is `pieceChain`, then `format=rgba,lut3d=file=<table>:interp=trilinear`, before crop, scale, opacity, fades, wipe mask and matte, on the bottom track (both sides of a dissolve, each its own grade) and on upper tracks. `RenderInput.grades`/`FrameInput.grades` name each table file by `gradeKey`; `gradeFiles` (`export/grade.ts`) reads each LUT once, writes nothing itself, and the service writes the `.cube` texts into the render or frame work directory, removed with it (no cache, nothing for `gc`). A LUT outside the project, missing or not a 3D `.cube` fails the request (`ExportUnsupported`, naming the clip); a graded clip without a table is drawn ungraded with a warning. Plans of timelines without grades are unchanged (every existing golden); `grade-plan` covers a graded dissolve. `grade-render.test.ts` runs the compiled frame through the managed ffmpeg against `applyGradeTable` on a random still: 0 of 18,432 channels differ on darwin-arm64.
- **Preview** (`preview/grade.ts`, `GradePass`, called from `#composite`): one WebGL2 `OffscreenCanvas` per engine, made with the first program that has a grade (context and shader take up to 0.4 s cold), not on the first graded frame; a program without grades never makes it. Per graded part: `texImage2D` of the decoded `VideoFrame` or still (no premultiply, flip or colour conversion on upload), one full-screen pass with the 8-tap shader, `transferToImageBitmap`, then the rest of `#composite` as before (alpha mask, placement, cover crop, shape) on that bitmap, closed after the draw. Parts without a grade take the old path: no extra copy. Tables are uploaded as `RGB32F` 3D textures when the program arrives and dropped when no program uses them. The renderer makes the tables (`useGradeTables`: LUTs read through the window, again when the file changes, last 32 tables kept) and `compileProgram` puts the ones in use in `Program.grades`, the span naming its table in `grade` (`tableKey`: the `gradeKey` and which table of it, so a LUT edited on disk gives a new key and the engine uploads the new table); the player sends each table to the engine once (`withNewTables`), not with every program; a grade whose LUT is not read yet or cannot be used draws ungraded, with a console warning. `EngineStats.graded` counts passes.
- **Fallback:** no WebGL2, a shader that does not compile, or a lost context: the part is drawn ungraded and the engine says so once (`gradeOff`); the preview shows a notice (`shared/i18n/preview.ts`, en + es). A restored context, its textures included, is rebuilt on the next graded frame.
- **Parity, measured:** `grade-preview.spec.ts` grades a 960x540 random still with a strong correction and a 17-point LUT and compares the preview canvas with `frameshell frame`: the test allows 1 level on under 0.1 % of channels; measured on darwin-arm64 (Apple M3, ANGLE Metal), no channel differs. `preview-grade.test.ts` checks the preview uploads the same table export writes, for the same clips. Video adds the chroma-upsampling difference of the second rule above.
- **Protocol 129:** `clip.set` takes `grade` (merged field by field; `lut: null` removes the LUT, `null` clears the grade; a `lut` path outside the project is refused); shots skip it with a warning. MCP `clip_set` follows from the registry. **CLI:** `clip set --grade-exposure`, `--grade-temperature`, `--grade-tint`, `--grade-contrast`, `--grade-saturation`, `--grade-lut <file | none>`, `--grade-lut-mix`, `--clear-grade`.
- `RECIPE_VERSION` and `CACHE_LAYOUT_VERSION` do not change; ADR 0001's thresholds are unchanged and its harness was not rerun with graded clips.
- **Not yet:** looks generated by Frameshell (decision 8), `grade.compare` (`grade.analyze` is slice 6), and grades on stills with alpha checked against export (the pass keeps straight alpha; not measured).

### Slice 5: Colour section of the clip inspector and the LOG warning (#384)

- **What it shows:** media clips on video tracks get a Colour group in the clip inspector (`components/ColourControl.tsx`, also in image projects): sliders for exposure, temperature, tint, contrast and saturation (`GRADE_LIMITS` ranges, in the order the grade applies them), a LUT menu and, while a LUT is set, its mix; Reset clears the grade. The sliders are native range inputs: dragging moves a local value and the browser's `change` (the release, or a key press) saves it, so a drag is one history step. Every save is one `clip.set` `grade` by `ui` with that one number (the daemon merges it), so undo, history and review mode work as for any edit. No wire change for the edits.
- **LUTs:** the `.cube` files of the project, from the window's file tree (`window.frameshell.files.tree`, again when files change; `grade/luts.ts`). The renderer writes nothing. A stored LUT the project no longer holds stays in the menu so the clip's value shows. Choosing "No LUT" sends `lut: null`.
- **LOG warning (decision 9):** warn, never refuse. `logFootageHint` (`packages/schema/src/grade.ts`) is a heuristic over what the probe holds, in order: `color_transfer` is HDR or logarithmic (`arib-std-b67`, `smpte2084`, `log100`, `log316`, `smpte428`), `color_primaries` are wide gamut (`bt2020`, `smpte431`, `smpte432`), or the file name has a camera LOG word (S-Log, C-Log, N-Log, F-Log, D-Log, J-Log, V-Log, LogC, HLG, a bare `log`; `vlog` without a dash is a video diary). Cameras often tag LOG as plain Rec.709, hence the name check; stills are never flagged. The inspector shows a note in the Colour group whenever the source looks like that (`asset.list` probe), and `clip.set` with a `grade` adds the same text as a result warning, which `clip set --grade-*` prints as `warning: ...` and MCP `clip_set` returns (`Engine.warnLogFootage` through `EditContext.source().log`; a source the context cannot read adds none). The grade is kept either way.
- **Probe:** `asset.list` and `MediaProbe` video carry `colorTransfer` and `colorPrimaries` (ffprobe tags, `null` when untagged or `unknown`), optional on the wire. A manifest without them is probed again by `MediaService.probe` (as for `alpha`), which the CLI warning uses; `asset.list` returns the manifest as stored, so the inspector's tag check starts for assets ingested from this version on, and an older asset is judged by its name alone until it is ingested again. **Protocol 130.**
- **Strings:** `shared/i18n/inspector.ts` (en + es). Tests: `packages/schema/test/grade.test.ts` (hint), `timeline-engine.test.ts` (warning), `alpha-probe.test.ts` (tags), `apps/desktop/test/grade-luts.test.ts`, `timeline-layout.test.ts`, e2e `colour-inspector.spec.ts` (sliders, one step per drag, LUT, reset, HLG note).

### Slice 6: `grade.analyze` (#384)

- **What it is:** `grade.analyze { timeline, clip, at?, from?, to?, samples?, view? }` measures the picture of a media clip on a video track and suggests a correction. It reads only: no file, no timeline change, no job; `view` is the review-mode read like `frame`'s. Not a mutating method, so no replay key. Code in `packages/core/src/grade/` (`levels.ts` parses and averages, `suggest.ts` is pure, `analyze.ts` runs ffmpeg and builds the result); wire in `packages/protocol/src/grade.ts`.
- **What it measures:** the clip's **source** frames, before its own grade, crop and placement (`GradeAnalyzer`, never the composed frame): so the suggestion is absolute for the source and replaces the clip's correction numbers, its LUT stays (the correction comes first, then the LUT). `clip.set` merges `grade` field by field, so a correction field the clip sets and the suggestion leaves out (it is within its target) is given in the suggestion, `flags` and `reasons` as its neutral value (exposure 0, temperature 0, tint 0, contrast 1, saturation 1); a clip with `exposure: 1` and a source on target gets `exposure 0`, not nothing. A clip that already has a grade says so in a warning and in `current`. Frames: `at` is one frame; `from`/`to` (timeline seconds inside the clip, default the clip's ends) take `samples` frames (default 8, 1 to 32) at the middle of equal parts, never more than the range holds at the project's frame rate; a still is one frame. Timeline seconds map to source seconds through the clip's `in` and `speed`. One ffmpeg run per frame (`-ss` before `-i`, 4 at once), so a long clip costs the seeks, not a pass over it. `at` and `from`/`to` may sit half a frame outside the clip (the reported `valid` is rounded to 3 decimals) and are clamped to it. A frame that cannot be read (ffmpeg fails or prints nothing) is left out with a warning; none at all is `InvalidOperation`.
- **How:** `scale=480:-2:flags=area:out_range=tv:out_color_matrix=bt709,format=yuv444p,signalstats,metadata=mode=print:file=-`: area-averaged to 480 px so a figure does not depend on the source's size, and brought to 8-bit video levels and Rec.709 whatever the file (a full-range file or an RGB photo reads like the picture the grade sees; a limited grey 128 reads Y 126). Luma is `(Y - 16) / 219` (0 studio black, 1 studio white); `low` and `high` are `signalstats`' 10th and 90th percentile; saturation is `SATAVG / 224`; warmth (mean red minus blue) and tint (mean green minus the mean of red and blue) come from the mean colour differences and are exact for the means because the conversion is linear. Over several frames every figure is the mean, `min` and `max` the extremes. `samples` (time, mean luma) shows a clip whose exposure moves.
- **The suggestion** (`SUGGEST` in `suggest.ts`, judgment and not colour science; a first pass from a grey-world reading that says what to look at, and never applies): exposure moves the mean to 0.45 in `2.4 * log2(0.45 / mean)` stops (the grade's exposure is a factor in light at gamma 2.4), at most 1 stop, never lifting the 90th percentile above 0.92, left alone under 0.15 stop; contrast takes the 10th to 90th percentile spread (after the exposure) to 0.55, within 0.8 to 1.25, never stretching either end into 0.97 or 0.02, left alone within 0.06 of 1; temperature and tint remove 60 % of the mean cast (a sunset is warm; never all of it), at most 0.4, left alone under a cast of 0.015 (about 4 of 255), sized by the controls' real effect (`0.167` and `0.0833` of the mean level per unit: gains `1 ± 0.2` in light, gamma 2.4; exposure and white balance multiply in the table, so the exposure gain cancels and the level is the measured mean, at least 0.15); saturation takes the mean colour strength, as the exposure and contrast suggested in the same call leave it (both scale the colour differences), to 0.15, within 0.85 to 1.2, left alone within 0.1 of 1 or under 0.02 (monochrome). A picture without range (black, or one flat colour) gets no suggestion and a line saying why; so does one within the targets. The tests apply the suggested white balance through the grade's own table and check the cast shrinks without overshooting, and that every suggestion parses as `clip.set` `grade`.
- **Result:** `levels`, `samples`, `current`, `suggestion` (the numbers that differ from neutral, in `clip.set` `grade` fields), `reasons` (one line per field), `flags` (the same as `clip set` flags, a negative number as `--grade-exposure=-0.4`), `warnings` (LOG or HDR footage by `logFootageHint`, decision 9: warn, never refuse; the levels of log footage are flat by design). Errors: `ClipNotFound` (no such clip on a video track), `InvalidOperation` (not a media clip, no picture, `at` with a range, a frame or range outside the clip with `data.valid`), `InvalidParams` (`samples` out of range).
- **Protocol 140.** CLI `frameshell grade analyze <clip> [--at s | --from s --to s] [--samples n]`; MCP `grade_analyze` follows from the registry; the skill's `cli.md`, `mcp.md` and `project-model.md` name it.
- **Limits:** the measure is of the whole source frame, not of the part the clip's crop shows, and of the source's own tags (no HDR tone mapping). A still with alpha is measured with its alpha dropped, so transparent pixels (often black) count in the levels. `levels.saturation` and `levels.warmth` are not `analyze`'s (HSV saturation, full-range RGB red minus blue): same names, other definitions, not comparable; the describe text says so. The targets are the same for every kind of picture: a night scene or a sunset is meant to read dark or warm, so the suggestion is to be judged by eye with `frame`.
- **Not yet:** `grade.compare` (candidates on one frame next to a neutral baseline) and looks generated by Frameshell (decision 8); a predicted picture after the suggestion.

### Slice 7: `grade.compare` (#384)

- **What it is:** `grade.compare { timeline, clip, looks, at?, out?, tileWidth?, projectFormat?, view? }` renders one frame of a media clip on a video track of the timeline (not inside a nested timeline) under 1 to 16 `looks` and writes them as one labelled PNG. HyperFrames' `grade-compare` is the design reference (#478). A look is a whole grade in the fields of `clip.set` `grade` plus an optional `name` (`GradeLookSchema`), drawn **in place of** the clip's grade, not merged with it: fields left out are neutral. Tiles in reading order: `1 neutral` (the clip with no grade), `2 current` (its stored grade, only when it has one that changes something, `gradeOf`), then the looks (`look <n>` when unnamed). A look that changes nothing is drawn and named in a warning. Not a mutating method, so no replay key; `view` is the review-mode read like `frame`'s. Code in `packages/core/src/grade/compare.ts` (`GradeComparer`); wire in `packages/protocol/src/grade.ts`.
- **How (no twin of frame capture):** each tile is `ExportService.frame` on a copy of the loaded timeline whose clip carries that tile's grade (`withGrade`; the timeline given is never mutated and nothing is written to the project's timeline), so the composed frame is what export draws: layers above, transitions, captions, letterbox, the chosen format. The first tile renders alone (it makes any generated render or matte the others then find cached), the rest `captureParallelism()` at once, into `.frameshell/cache/grade-compare/<random>/`, removed afterwards. The tiles go through `writeSheet` (`review/sheet.ts`, the sheet `review` and `analyze` use: area scale, burned labels in the bundled face, `tile`). Tile width `tileWidth` (160 to 960, default 480), height from the frame, even; one row up to 4 tiles, else `ceil(sqrt(n))` columns, so 18 tiles of a vertical film stay under about 5,000 px a side.
- **Frame:** `at` (timeline seconds) picks the frame that holds it, with half a frame of slack at the clip's ends as `grade.analyze`; default the clip's middle frame. `out` (absolute or relative to `cwd`) defaults to `.frameshell/grade/compare-<clip>-<frame>.png`, replaced by the next call on that frame.
- **Keeping a look:** each tile's `flags` turn the clip's grade into that tile's (`lookFlags`): `--clear-grade` for the baseline; a look's fields plus the neutral value of each field the clip's grade sets and the look leaves out, because `clip.set` merges `grade` field by field; empty for `current`. The CLI prints one `frameshell clip set` line per tile.
- **Errors:** `ClipNotFound` (no such clip on a video track), `InvalidOperation` (not a media clip; `at` outside the clip, with `data.valid`), `InvalidParams` (a look's LUT outside the project, missing or not a 3D `.cube`, checked by `readLut` before any frame renders, naming the look; more than 16 looks), and what `frame` fails with. A hidden track gets a warning: every tile then looks the same.
- **Protocol 146.** CLI `frameshell grade compare <clip> --look [name:]field=value[,...] [--look ...] [--at s] [--out file.png] [--tile-width px] [--format id]` (fields `exposure`, `temperature`, `tint`, `contrast`, `saturation`, `lut`, `lut-mix`); MCP `grade_compare` follows from the registry and returns the sheet as an image (scaled to fit 1568 px) with the facts. Tests: `packages/core/test/grade-compare.test.ts` (flags, layout, swap; a flat grey still through the managed ffmpeg, centre pixel of every tile: neutral, brighter, warmer, a LUT to red, the clip's own grade; timeline file byte-identical; errors), `packages/cli/test/grade-compare-cli.test.ts`.
- **Not yet:** a `grade.analyze` suggestion added as a look by itself (today: pass it as a look). The built-in looks of decision 8 came in slice 10.

### Slice 8: grade on generated clips and nested timeline clips (#384)

- **Schema:** adapter (generated) and nested timeline clips take the same `grade` as media clips (`GradeSchema`, JSON Schema regenerated; older hosts reject files that carry it). Shots still take none. `gradeOf` reads any clip but a shot.
- **Generated clips:** the render is graded like footage, first in the part: export's `gradedChain` runs `format=rgba,lut3d` right after the render is read (libvpx keeps the VP9 alpha plane, `lut3d` passes alpha through), then crop, scale, opacity, fades, shape; the preview grades the decoded colour frame in the WebGL2 pass before `AlphaMasker` applies the render's alpha plane, so the table always sees straight colour, as the spike asked (`webgl2-grade.md` rule 6). The per-part order is ADR 0043's: grade → vignette → grain → shape → mask → backdrop. A live page (ADR 0010) that plays instead of the render shows ungraded, as it shows no backdrop. Render caches are untouched (`cache-key.ts` reads only type, source, props and elements).
- **Nested timeline clips (table composition):** flattening records the grade of each nested clip a picture plays inside on that picture (`hostGrades`, innermost first, plain data like `nesting`; `recordHostGrade` in `composite.ts`), and `gradeChainOf` returns the clip's own grade, then those, without grades that change nothing. Export and the preview make **one table per chain** (`gradeChainTable`): at every lattice point each grade's correction and LUT in turn on the exact values the one before left, as large as the largest LUT; a chain of one is the old `gradeTable` and keeps its `gradeKey` (`gradeChainKey`), so the plans and file names of timelines without nested grades are unchanged. So a picture of a nested timeline is drawn through its own grade, then its hosts', before its own vignette and grain; overlapping pictures of the nested timeline are each graded before they are composited, as a nested clip's opacity multiplies per layer (`composePlacement`), not on the mix of them. Sound clips get no host grade; shots of a nested program track take none (decision 5). `gradeFiles` reads every LUT of a chain once and names the clip when one cannot be used (its own LUT, or a nested clip's it plays inside).
- **Preview:** `useGradeTables` flattens the shown timeline with its nested views (`flattenView`, the flattening `compileProgram` does) to find the chains, reads each chain's LUTs and makes the same tables; generated spans carry `grade` like media and stills. Nothing else in the engine changed.
- **Operation:** `clip.set` `grade` (protocol 153) is taken by media, generated and nested timeline clips on video tracks; audio tracks are refused, shots skipped with the warning as before. The LOG footage warning stays on media clips. CLI `clip set --grade-*` and MCP `clip_set` are unchanged.
- **Tests:** `packages/schema/test/grade.test.ts` (chain order, keys, one table equal to the same grade done at once, twice a red-blue swap is the identity, size), `composite.test.ts` (chains of flattened clips two levels deep, none on sound); goldens `grade-generated-plan` (a VP9 alpha title and an opaque end card) and `grade-nested-plan` (the plan of a graded nested clip is the ungraded plan plus the `lut3d` steps); `grade-render.test.ts` through the managed ffmpeg: a nested still under a two-grade chain against `applyGradeTable` of the chain's table (at most 1 level on under 0.1 % of channels), and a VP9 alpha render on an upper track: the graded frame equals the ungraded one through the table within 1 level where opaque, and the picture below is untouched where transparent (the bottom track draws renders opaque, as before); `apps/desktop/test/preview-grade.test.ts`: generated and nested spans carry their chain's key, and the preview's tables equal the `.cube` texts export writes for the same flattened timeline.
- **Not yet:** an e2e pixel check of a graded render in the preview. (`grade.analyze` and `grade.compare` took generated and nested clips in slice 11.)

### Slice 10: built-in looks (#384)

- **What they are:** six looks, `warm`, `cool`, `filmic`, `punchy`, `faded`, `mono`, as plain grade numbers (exposure, temperature, tint, contrast, saturation; no LUT) in one pure table, `GRADE_LOOKS` in `packages/schema/src/grade-looks.ts` (`findLook`, `lookGrade`, `lookNames`; no Zod, the renderer imports it through `@frameshell/schema/grade-looks`). Each has a name, a one-line summary and a whole correction inside `GRADE_LIMITS` that changes colours. The numbers are judgment, not colour science: a starting point for Rec.709 footage, to compare by eye and adjust.
- **Why numbers and not LUTs (decision 8):** no `.cube` file is generated or bundled, so there is no file to version, no licence question and no third-party look. Numbers go through the same one table export and the preview already apply (`gradeTable`), so nothing in export, preview or the timeline model changed and no parity test was needed. A generated 33-point `.cube` would hold nothing the five numbers do not.
- **Applying one copies it:** `clip set --grade-look <name>` writes the look's five numbers into the clip's explicit `grade` (the `--glass` pattern: the table is where the default comes from, the clip holds its own copy). A later change to the table never moves a clip that took a look, and timelines stay byte-identical. Explicit `--grade-*` flags win over the look; the clip's LUT stays. The inspector's Look menu in the Colour group does the same in one undoable `clip.set` `grade`.
- **Wire (protocol 174):** `grade.looks` (read, no params, no project: the table with names, summaries and grades). `grade.compare` looks take `look: "<name>"` as a base that fills the fields left out (the fields given win, the tile is named after the look unless it has a `name`), resolved in `GradeComparer` before any frame renders (`resolveLooks`); an unknown name is `InvalidParams` listing the valid ones.
- **CLI:** `frameshell grade looks`; `clip set --grade-look <name>`; `grade compare --look warm` (a bare built-in name) and `--look mine:look=warm,saturation=1.1`. MCP `grade_looks` follows from the registry.
- **Tests:** `packages/schema/test/grade-looks.test.ts` (each look parses as a `Grade`, inside the limits, not identity, a finite table that never reverses a grey ramp), `packages/core/test/grade-compare.test.ts` (a look by name draws the same centre pixel as its fields written out, unknown name error, `grade.looks` equals the table), `packages/cli/test/grade-looks-cli.test.ts`, `packages/protocol/test/methods.test.ts`.
- **Not yet:** a `grade.analyze` suggestion kept as a look by name; user looks saved in the project (a LUT file does that today).

### Slice 11: grade.analyze and grade.compare on generated and nested clips (#384)

- **Decision (owner, 2026-11-18):** `grade.analyze` measures a generated clip and refuses one whose render has alpha and a nested timeline clip; `grade.compare` accepts all three kinds. No new decision of this ADR changed: compare is the same frame path, analyze reads the render file where it read the source file.
- **`grade.compare`:** accepts media, generated and nested timeline clips (a shot is `InvalidOperation` `not-gradable`, the old `not-media` reason is gone). `withGrade` swaps the grade of any clip but a shot; `current` is read by `gradeOf` of the clip as it is. The frame to draw comes from `clipEnd` (the engine's own end of a clip, so a nested clip with no `duration` plays the rest of its file after `in`, read through `TimelineService.nestedDuration`), not a private length rule. Each tile is `ExportService.frame`, so a generated clip is drawn from its cached render (the draft when the final is not made, as `frame` says) and what the adapter said about it comes back in `warnings`.
- **`grade.analyze`:** a generated clip is measured from its **draft** render (`ClipRenderer.ensure` with `tier: "draft"`, interactive, cached; the call waits for a missing one as `frame` does) at render time `in + (time - start)` through the same `statsAt`, `parseSignalstats` and `suggestGrade` as footage. The render is the clip's picture before its own grade, the same meaning as a source file; it is asked of the cache for that clip alone (the other generated clips are taken off a copy of the timeline; tempo, markers and the other tracks stay, so the key is the one `frame` makes). A render with alpha is refused (`has-alpha`: `signalstats` would count the empty parts as black); a nested clip is refused (`nested`) pointing at the clips inside and at `grade.compare`. The draft is sharp and a blurred final has the same levels, so there is no motion-blur warning; the LOG hint stays on media. Reads only: it writes no project file, but a missing render is made and cached.
- **Wire (protocol 177):** both results gain `kind` (`media`, `generated`, `timeline`; analyze never `timeline`) and `asset` becomes nullable (a render has no project asset). No param changed. Older clients that read `asset` as a string must accept null.
- **Surfaces:** CLI reports name a clip with no source file by kind (`generated clip`, `nested timeline`); MCP follows the registry; skill and docs say what each method takes.
- **Tests:** `grade-compare.test.ts` (swap on generated and nested clips, a shot refused, tile colours of a card and of a nested still by the real frame path, timeline file untouched, nested frame from the nested file), `grade-analyze.test.ts` (stubbed refusals and the timeline the render cache is asked for; an opaque card measured, an alpha card and a nested clip refused), the CLI tests; the `card` fixture takes `opaque: true`.
- **Not yet:** a nested clip's picture is not measured (analyze the clips inside it); an alpha render is not measured over a neutral backdrop.

## Consequences

- An effect change takes seconds to bake before the preview hears it; the dry sound plays meanwhile. Export always waits for the bake.
- The bake is mono, like the sidecar, so a stereo clip with effects plays as mono in export. Voice, which the chain is for, is mono.
- Compression and denoise change the mix before `loudnorm`; the final level is still `export.loudness`. `transcribe --verify` of an export hears the processed sound.
- Long chains stay out of the big mix graph that ADR 0005 found fragile.
- The effect kinds are a closed set: a new kind needs core code. Plugin effects (SPEC §8.2, v0.3) can map onto these kinds later without a model break.
- Disk: one 48 kHz mono s16 file per asset and chain, about 165 MiB per 30 minutes, regenerable.
