# Timeline markers and beat grids measured per asset

Films are timed in beats and reels in words (#458, epic #451). Frameshell had words but neither markers nor a beat grid; ADR 0023 left markers out on purpose ("a new timeline concept"). This ADR adds both: markers on the timeline clock, and a beat grid measured per music asset and mapped onto the timeline through the music clip. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-10 in #458 (option A of the proposed decision, every recommendation accepted); the beat grid lives in core as decided in #478. It shipped in slices, all built: 1 (markers), 2 (the beat detector, `beats.measure|show` and the beats file), 3 (the grid on the timeline) and 4 (TimeRef names: seconds and markers, then `beat:N` and `bar:N`, then `word:` refs), with decision 8 (timing for generated clips: beats, markers and words).

## Context (verified)

- `TimelineSchema` (`packages/schema/src/timeline.ts`) is a strict object; `SCHEMA_VERSION` 1 has taken every additive field (`groups`, `keys`) without a bump.
- Inverses are diff patches (ADR 0004): `TimelinePatchSchema` (`packages/protocol/src/timeline.ts`) covered only `groups`, `tracks`, `clips` and `order`, so a top-level field needs its own patch entry or revert silently stops restoring it.
- Review mode (ADR 0019) decides staleness and rebase from the entity keys a patch touches (`packages/core/src/history/revert.ts`).
- Snapping in the app: `SnapPoint` with kinds `clip` and `playhead` (`renderer/src/timeline/edit.ts`); in core, the `EditPointResolver` seam that energy snapping (ADR 0003) uses for `cut` and `clip.trim`.
- The scene band (`renderer/src/timeline/scenes.ts`) already shows named spans under the ruler; markers are points, not spans.
- `EnergyStore` caches 10 ms envelopes; too coarse for ±8 ms beat snapping, and a different signal (RMS, not spectral flux).

## Decision

1. **Markers ripple with `cut`** like clip starts: markers after the cut move with the material, markers inside the removed range are deleted (named in `warnings`; `revert` brings them back). A marker on the cut's first frame stays: it marks the new edit point (confirmed by the owner in #458 after the slice 1 review, as is the next rule). Only a `cut` of every video, audio and program track moves or deletes markers; a cut of some tracks shifts clips against the rest, so markers stay where they are, because they belong to the whole timeline. A `ripple` `clip.trim` treats removed material like a `cut`: markers strictly inside the removed head or tail are deleted (a marker on the first removed frame stays), later ones shift, and the same every-track rule holds. `ripple` inserts and the growing side of a trim (the inverse of `cut`) shift markers at or after the insertion point like clip starts, so restoring cut material puts them back in step.
2. **Timeline markers only in v1.** `markers: [{ id: "m_<hex>", at, label, color? }]`, optional, on the timeline clock, `at` on the frame grid, ids unique among the file's ids, labels unique per timeline ignoring case (so a later `--at hook` resolves to one) and never shaped like a marker id (`m_` plus hex), so a reference is an id when it matches one and a label otherwise, never both, `color` one of `red`, `orange`, `yellow`, `green`, `blue`, `purple` (the app paints each from its theme; absent: the accent). Clip-anchored markers come later.
3. **Beat corrections live in a versioned project file** `beats/<stem>.beats.json` (asset, asset hash, detector, BPM, confidence, beats, downbeat phase, alternatives, human `edits` such as half/double time or phase), like transcripts; regenerable onset data lives under `.frameshell/`; `gc` never sweeps `beats/`. The watcher and direct-edit reconcile handle `beats/` The file's schema is defined once, in slice 2, with a `detector` field that admits `exact` (a grid known rather than measured); no consumer (grid moves of `music.fit`, ADR 0037; music made by `sound`, #463) defines its own (cross rule 3 on #451, 2026-11-11).
4. **The timeline grid comes from one chosen music clip** (`tempo.clip`), with a nominal `bpm`/`offset` fallback for blocking a film before music exists; the schema checks the referenced clip exists.
5. **Markers of a nested timeline stay inside it** in v1.
6. **Energy snapping stays first** for `cut` and `trim`; snapping to beats or markers is opt-in per operation (`--snap-to beat|marker`). Beats never snap on low confidence. In the app, a dragged edge snaps to markers like it does to clip edges and the playhead.
7. **Names as times** (`--at hook`, `beat:32`, `bar:8`), resolved in the daemon. This ADR owns the shared **TimeRef** contract that voiceover and motion reuse; word refs (`"this#2"`, `"over+0.2"`) are a later slice of the same contract.
   *Amended 2026-11-11 (#485, ADR 0037):* the resolver is built first for **seconds and markers** (a marker id or label, with an optional `+s`/`-s` offset), as the first piece of slice 4, because `music.fit`, sound placement and voiceover placement need it before the beat grid exists. `beat:`, `bar:` and word refs join the same resolver (`SCHEMES` in `packages/core/src/timeline/time-ref.ts`) and are refused until then. `music.fit`, `sound` and voiceover placement (ADR 0034 decision 15) use it; none gets a private parser. See ADR 0037 "Slice 1 as built" for the contract and the arguments that take it.
   *Amended 2026-11-17 (owner, Q4 of #458):* word refs are written `word:<text>[#n][+s|-s]`, the start of the nth time the timeline plays the word, matched on its spoken tokens, or a transcript word id; the bare quoted forms (`"this#2"`) are not accepted. See "Slice 4 part 3 as built".
8. **Generated clips get beat and marker times** through an opt-in `RenderContext` timing field that is part of the cache key: only clips that ask re-render when the music moves.
9. **One FFT.** The radix-2 FFT of `multicam/sync.ts` moves to one shared module used by sync and beats.
10. **Learned detector only on failure.** If the DSP detector misses a grid residual under 15 ms on synthetic click tracks or fails on real music, run the learned-detector spike (Beat This! or similar on ONNX Runtime Web) after a licence check of its weights (madmom's are non-commercial). Owner decision 2026-11-18 (Q1 of #458): the real-music check and this spike moved to #689.

From the review: the detector resamples the 48 kHz sidecar to its analysis rate; a result from the 16 kHz ffmpeg fallback is marked low confidence.

### Slice 1 as built (markers)

- Operations `marker.add`, `marker.set`, `marker.remove` (journaled, inverse patches, idempotent replays), read method `marker.list`; `timeline.show` lists markers. A marker is named by id or by label (ignoring case). Protocol 71.
- `TimelinePatchSchema` gains `markers` (upsert or delete by id); review mode and revert key them as `marker:<id>`, so a person's change to a proposal's marker makes the proposal stale, and a change to another marker rebases it.
- Direct edits of the timeline file go through the same rules: duplicate ids or labels, an id-shaped label and a time off the frame grid are rejected into `.frameshell/rejected/` (the schema, and `timeline.patch` for the grid); a journaled edit is written back sorted by `at`.
- CLI `frameshell marker add --at s --label l [--color c] | set <marker> | remove <marker> | list`; MCP tools from the registry.
- App: a marker lane under the ruler (shown while there are markers), a faint guide down the lanes, add, rename and delete from the ruler's right-click menu, `SnapPoint` kind `marker`; a trimmed edge put on a marker goes exactly (no energy snap), as on a clip edge.

### Slice 2 as built (beat detector and beats file)

- **Detector** (`packages/core/src/media/beats.ts`, pure, `onset-flux@1`): the recipe measured in #458. The source is resampled to 22.05 kHz (windowed sinc) and a spectral-flux onset envelope taken (window 2048, hop 128, log magnitude, 100 ms of silent pre-roll). Its peaks under 0.12 of the strongest are dropped and each remaining one is refined to the sample where a 0.5 ms average of the 30 Hz-high-passed signal rises 20% of the way from the energy minimum before it: the envelope alone is 20-50 ms late. Tempo is the autocorrelation peak of the envelope (interpolated lags, 35-340 BPM), weighted by a prior (the hint, else 120 BPM), then moved by octaves to the hint's octave, or into 70-170 BPM with no hint. Phase is the comb at 5 ms steps that collects most hit energy; one straight period is then fitted by weighted least squares over the hits, the window widening until it spans the audio, so a long track does not drift. Each beat snaps to a hit within 8 ms; the bar starts at the phase (of 4) with most 20-150 Hz energy in the 0.25 s after its beats. The grid runs from the first matched hit to the last.
- **Confidence** is `share^1.5 * (0.6 + 0.4 * periodic)`: `share` is how many beats between the first and last matched found a hit within 30 ms, `periodic` how strong the envelope's autocorrelation is at that tempo. Clicks on every second beat (calm music) fold into a double-time grid with `share` about 0.5, which is LOW (under 0.5); the file then lists `alternatives` (`double`, `half`, best supported first) and a human picks one with `edits.tempo`. The 16 kHz fallback decode (no finished ingest; transcription never waits for ingest either) caps confidence at 0.45. On synthetic click tracks the beats come out within 15 ms of the clicks (a few ms in the tests), at half time, double time, with a hint, with missing clicks and a noise bed, over 150 s at a non-round 127.6 BPM.
- **Where it runs**: a worker thread (`media/beats-worker.ts`, the shared `worker-task.ts` runner), so the daemon's event loop is never blocked; about 5 s for a 3-minute track. Tracks over 10 minutes are refused (a grid is for music). The onset envelope, which is most of the cost, is cached by content hash and analysis rate in `.frameshell/beats/<hash>-b1-<rate>.f32` (`BeatsStore`, modelled on `EnergyStore`, which stays a separate store: a 10 ms RMS dB envelope is too coarse and a different signal); measuring again with another hint reuses it. `gc` sweeps it once no asset has that content (`classifyBeatsCache`, kind `beats`) and never touches `beats/`.
- **One FFT**: the radix-2 FFT of `multicam/sync.ts` moved to `packages/core/src/fft.ts` (twiddle table per size), used by sync and the detector (decision 9). `people/fbank.ts` keeps its fixed 512-point transform, pinned to its reference by test. The per-asset file naming of transcripts moved to `asset-names.ts` and is shared with beats.
- **The beats file** `beats/<stem>.beats.json` (SPEC §5.9, `BeatsFileSchema`, JSON Schema `beats.json`) is defined once for every writer: `detector` is `exact` (a generator that knows its beats, #463; no `sampleRate`, `residualMs` or `onsets` needed) or `<id>@<version>` (cross rule 3 of #451). `edits` are `tempo` (`double`, `half`), `downbeat` and `offset`, applied by one `effectiveBeatGrid`. Same naming and ownership as transcripts: the name whose file records the asset, else the first free one, never overwritten for another asset (`InvalidOperation`, reason `name taken`). Measuring again keeps `edits` unless `resetEdits`.
- **Direct edits** of `beats/` (decision 3): `file.write` validates `beats/**/*.beats.json` like transcripts and keeps the old file when the new one is invalid. Slice 2 had no watcher or reconcile for it, because nothing cached the file: `beats.show` reads it on every call, and an editor that writes it directly is read on the next call (a broken file is `InvalidProjectFile`). Slice 3 adds the watcher, now that a change must repaint the app (see "Slice 3 as built").
- **Methods**: `beats.measure` (mutating, a background job of kind `beats`, step `beats`; params `asset`, `bpm` hint, `resetEdits`) and `beats.show` (read; the grid with `edits` applied, `current` false when the asset changed since); CLI `frameshell beats measure|show`; MCP `beats_measure`, `beats_show`. Protocol 86.
- **Nominal grid**: `nominalBeatFrame(i, bpm, fps, offset)` rounds each beat once from its index (a rounded step added up drifts 2 frames in 32 beats at 128 BPM and 30 fps, pinned by test). Slice 3 uses it for the grid before music exists.
- Not in this slice: `tempo`, the grid in the app, `--snap-to` (slice 3, below); TimeRef (slice 4); a learned detector (decision 10, only if real music fails; #689); downbeat confidence; a meter other than 4.

### Slice 3 as built (the grid on the timeline)

- **`tempo` in the timeline file** (`TempoSchema`, SPEC §5.3), optional, `schemaVersion` stays 1 (an older Frameshell rejects a timeline that carries it, like `markers`). Three shapes, one object: `{ clip }` (the measured grid of the beats file of that clip's asset), `{ bpm, offset?, meter? }` (nominal, on the timeline clock) and `{ clip, bpm, offset? }` (nominal, riding on the clip, `offset` in its source seconds, for music that has no beats file yet). `bpm` is 20 to 400. The schema checks that `tempo.clip` names a media clip of a video or audio track (decision 4), so a direct edit that drops the music but keeps `tempo` is rejected like any invalid edit.
- **One resolver** (`packages/core/src/timeline/tempo.ts`, `tempoGrid`): `effectiveBeatGrid` (so a file's `edits` apply) carried through the clip, `start + (beat - in) / speed`, for beats in `[in, out)`, each rounded once to the frame grid; a nominal grid is `nominalBeatFrame(i, bpm, fps, offset)` (on a clip: with `bpm × speed` and the offset carried through the clip). Beats and bars (`bars` is the subset that starts a bar) come out in timeline seconds; a nominal grid on the timeline clock runs to the last requested time plus a bar (at least 2 beats), so a time near the end still finds the next bar. At most 20 000 beats. `tempo.show` and `timeline.show` with `grid: true` read this and nothing else computes a grid; the app draws arrays from the daemon.
- **Operation `tempo.set`** (`clip?`, `bpm?`, `offset?`, `meter?`, `clear?`; replaces the whole tempo; journaled; the inverse patch has a `tempo` entry, one value that review mode and revert key as the entity `tempo`). Removing the followed clip (`clip.remove`, `track.remove`, a `cut`) clears the tempo with a warning, and the inverse restores both; `clip.split` keeps it on the left part and warns. Read method `tempo.show`; `timeline.show` lists the stored `tempo`. Protocol 96.
- **`snapTo` and `--snap-to beat|bar` (and `marker`, below)** (decision 6, opt-in per operation): every operation with a time reference (`TIME_REF_FIELDS`) takes it; the daemon resolves the times, moves each to the nearest beat or bar start in the operation's turn (after any `prepare`, against the timeline as it is then) and records the seconds. A `cut` or `clip.trim` snapped to a beat goes exactly there: pause snapping is off unless the caller passes `snap: true`. Beats never snap on low confidence, so a LOW grid is refused (a file with human `edits` vouches for itself, `isLowConfidence`), as is a missing tempo, a clip with no beats file yet, an operation with no time argument given and a time farther than a beat (a bar, for `bar`) outside the grid. `beat:N` and `bar:N` as names are slice 4 (below).
- **The grid in the app**: a faint line per beat and a stronger one per bar down the lanes (under the clips) and ticks on the ruler's bottom edge, drawn from `timeline.show`'s `grid`; a level too dense for the zoom (under 6 px) is left out, bars alone at medium zoom. Drags snap to beats and bars (`SnapPoint` kinds `beat` and `bar`) after the clip edges, markers and the playhead: those win when in reach, because beats are everywhere; an edge put on a beat goes exactly (no pause snap), like on a marker. A LOW grid is drawn but offers no snap points. The app only reads the tempo in this slice: setting it is `tempo set` (the person or an agent); a control in the app comes with the music flow that needs it. Strings in `shared/i18n/timeline.ts` (en and es); e2e `tempo-grid.spec.ts`.
- **Watcher and reconcile of `beats/`** (decision 3): `BeatsWatcher` watches `beats/` of every opened project, also when the folder does not exist yet, and after a write settles validates the file with the schema `file.write` and `beats.measure` share and sends `beats.changed` (`written`, `removed` or `invalid` with the message). The beats file has no daemon-side state to reconcile (it is read on every use), so reconcile means telling clients: the app reads every timeline again on `beats.changed`, so a hand edit of `edits.tempo`, `edits.downbeat` or `edits.offset` changes the grid at once, and an invalid file takes the grid away (`tempo.show` says why) until it validates again. An invalid file is never moved or rewritten (unlike a timeline, there is no daemon version to restore, and the person's edits are what it holds).
- **`--snap-to marker`** (decision 6 names `beat|marker`; `bar` is added): the nearest marker of the timeline within 1 s (`MARKER_SNAP_REACH_S`), through the same `snapRequest` as `beat` and `bar`. It needs no tempo, and `marker.set` never offers the marker it moves. A marker name as a time (`--at hook`) says "this marker's time"; `--snap-to marker` says "the marker nearest to this time", so the two stay different things.
- Not in this slice: setting the tempo from the app; `beat:` and `bar:` names, word refs and the TimeRef contract for them (slice 4); the opt-in `RenderContext` timing field for generated clips (decision 8, built later: see "Decision 8 as built"); the marker/beat snap in `cut` and `clip.trim` together with the energy resolver (`snapTo` turns pause snapping off unless the caller passes `snap: true`, which the CLI does not offer; the CLI refuses `--snap-window` with `--snap-to`); the meter of a measured grid other than the file's.

### Slice 4 as built (`beat:N` and `bar:N`)

Seconds and markers were built first with ADR 0037 slice 1 (see decision 7's amendment). This part adds the beat grid names to the same resolver; no verb has a private parser.

- **Forms.** `beat:N` is the Nth beat of the timeline's grid, `bar:N` the start of the Nth bar, both **counting from 1** (`bar:1` is the first bar start, the way people count bars; `beat:0` and `bar:0` are refused with a pointer). `N` may be fractional (`beat:2.5` is halfway between beats 2 and 3, `bar:2.5` halfway between the starts of bars 2 and 3), interpolated along the grid's own beats, so a measured grid with a drifting tempo is followed rather than extrapolated. The optional offset is the marker one: `bar:8-0.2`, `beat:17+0.1` (seconds). The scheme is case-insensitive. A marker label shaped like `beat:<number>` or `bar:<number>` (any case, so a marker can never shadow a grid reference) is refused on write (`marker.add`, `marker.set`: the engine's `claimLabel`, not the `MarkerSchema`, so a project saved earlier with such a label still loads and a revert or a direct file edit that carries one is tolerated). On read the grid wins: a `beat:<number>` / `bar:<number>` reference always means the grid, even if an old marker has that label (it stays reachable by id). `beat:drop` is an ordinary label.
- **One grid.** The resolver reads `tempoGrid` (`timeline/tempo.ts`, slice 3) for the timeline in the state the operation sees; there is no second grid. Numbers count in the **music file**, not the clip (owner decision, #550): `beat:1` is the file's first measured beat, `bar:1` its first downbeat, so trimming the head of the music clip never renumbers (`bar:8` stays the same musical bar). `TimelineGrid` carries `beatBase` / `barBase`, the entries of the file before the first one the clip shows. With a pickup (beats before the first downbeat) `bar:N` is not `beat:(meter*(N-1)+1)`: bars count from the downbeat. A nominal grid is `nominalBeatFrame`; on a clip it counts from the nominal origin (`offset`). Beats and bars are already on the frame grid, so `beat:32` lands on a frame without rounding an added step.
- **Refusals** use the words of `--snap-to` (`unusableGrid` in `time-ref.ts` is the one text): no `tempo`, a clip with no beats file yet, a broken beats file (`InvalidProjectFile`), a LOW confidence grid (a file with human `edits` vouches for itself), and `N` < 1. Past the measured part (or before the clip's first shown beat) a number is **extrapolated** by the listed entries' average period instead of refused, so `bar:200` of a 60 s clip lands where the song would put it; a result before 0 is refused. `music.fit` `--end`/`--from` go through the same async resolver (the sync `resolveTimeRefArg` is gone), reading the clip as it is before the fit; a review-mode proposal timeline resolves against its own markers and grid.
- **Where it runs.** `resolveTimeRefs` became asynchronous and takes a grid loader; the loader is called only when a reference is a `beat:`/`bar:`, and once for all the fields of an operation. `TimelineService` owns the loader (new option `beatsOf`, set by the daemon from `ownBeatsFile`) and passes it in the operation's turn, so a transaction that sets the tempo and then places a clip on `bar:3` sees the new grid, and review mode resolves against the proposal timeline. `snapRequest` resolves through the same function, so `--snap-to bar` with `--at beat:5` works. The journal records seconds, as for markers: a later change to the tempo never moves a recorded operation.
- **Every method that took a TimeRef takes it**: the `TIME_REF_FIELDS` table is unchanged; CLI and MCP send the text and the daemon resolves it. The CLI accepts the text for every time flag and the help describes it. The app's own edits send seconds.
- **Wire**: the `TimeRef` description names the new forms (model-facing); the argument types do not change. Protocol 97; #550 (protocol 107) counts from the music file and extrapolates.
- **Word refs** (`"this#2"`, `"over+0.2"`) stay a separate owner decision and are refused with a pointer.

### Slice 4 part 2 as built (the remaining time arguments)

Every argument that still took plain seconds on the timeline clock now takes a TimeRef. Same resolver, same refusals; no verb gets a private parser.

- **Operations** (the `TIME_REF_FIELDS` table): `multicam.reframe` `time`. `at` of that operation is the group clock and stays seconds.
- **Methods that read times of their own**: `sound.place` (`cues[].at`, `typing.from`, `typing.to`) and `sound.music` (`at`, `sections[].at`, `accents[]`) already declared the TimeRef type but resolved markers only (a sync resolver with no grid), so a `bar:N` was refused as "needs the beat grid". They now resolve through `resolveTimeRefAsync` with the grid of the timeline as it is before the call (the new bed's own grid does not exist yet; with no `tempo` such a reference is refused, so use seconds or a marker for the first bed). `captions.set` `from`/`to`, `multicam.direct` `from`/`to` and `ui.seek` `at` are new TimeRef params; the daemon resolves them with `resolveTimeRefArg`, which names the method and argument in the refusal, and passes seconds on (the journal of `multicam.direct` shows `from`/`to` in seconds). `ui.seek` reads a name on the timeline the app shows (`ui.state` `timeline`), as the person sees it, and still fails with `UiNotConnected` when no window shows the project.
- **One grid per call**: `gridLoaderFor` (daemon) reads the beat grid once per call and again only when a reference reaches further, shared by `voiceover.place`, `sound.place` and `sound.music`, which place many times in one call.
- **Left as numbers on purpose**: group-clock times (`multicam.reframe` `at`, `multicam.paint` `start`/`end`, turns), source-clock `in`/`out`, the curves of the `track.duck` and `music.shape` operations (computed by the daemon from measured speech and hits, never typed by a caller; the hits and `ending` that shape them already take TimeRefs), and the `snapTo` option, which `multicam.reframe` does not offer.
- **Wire**: protocol 149 (argument types of five methods widen from number to number or text).

### Slice 4 part 3 as built (word refs)

Owner decision 2026-11-17 (Q4 of #458): word refs join the same resolver as `word:` names, on the timeline clock, matched on spoken words; no verb gets a private parser.

- **Form.** `word:<text>[#n][+s|-s]` is the start of the nth time (from 1, counted on the timeline clock, default 1) the timeline plays the word, plus the offset. There is no `.start`/`.end`: the reference is the word's start, and an offset reaches past it (`word:over+0.2`). A transcript word id (`word:w_000012`) names that word wherever it plays (`#n` counts its placements); it is refused when two assets on the timeline have a word with that id. The bare quoted forms of the ticket (`"this#2"`, `"over+0.2"`) are **not** accepted: a bare word would compete with marker labels, and a misspelt label would silently become a word. The scheme is case-insensitive. A marker label starting with `word:` is refused on write like a grid one (`isSchemeRefLabel` replaced `isGridRefLabel`); on read the scheme wins.
- **Matching.** Spoken tokens (`spokenTokens` in `packages/schema/src/spoken.ts`, the one normalization of the aligner and the take comparison): case, accents and punctuation ignored, digits spelled in the transcript's language (`word:20` meets "twenty", `word:twenty` meets "20"), the parts of a hyphenated word apart, so `twenty-three`, "twenty three" said as two words and `23` meet. Tokens are compared one for one, never glued: `word:maybe` does not meet "may be", nor `word:in to` "into" (a split compound would move `#n` silently). Text of several words matches a run of consecutive played words of one track (`word:thank you`); runs are matched per track before copies on other tracks fold, so two mics never interleave a phrase. A word corrected in the transcript's `edits` matches by its corrected text. Both readings of text with a trailing `+s`/`-s` are tried: the whole text as words (`word:covid-19`) and the text before it plus the offset. When both give a time, the reference is refused naming both and how to write each (`word:chapter 2`, `word:chapter#1-2`); when neither does, both are named.
- **Which words.** `placeWords` (`packages/core/src/transcripts/timeline-words.ts`), extracted from `cleanup`'s `gatherSpeech` so both read one placement: unmuted media clips of every track but subtitles (program tracks folded into their angles' sound, `withProgramSound`) as they play after overlaps are trimmed; a word belongs to the clip that plays most of it (at least 0.15 s, or half of a shorter word), at the clip's mapping of its start through `start`, `in` and `speed`. Words a cut removed (struck in the transcript view) are not played and never counted. The same speech heard on two tracks at once (two mics, a clip's sound on two tracks) counts once: an occurrence that overlaps the one before is folded into it. Words inside a nested timeline clip are not placed on its host, as for `cleanup`.
- **Refusals.** A heard clip whose transcript is older than its file refuses every `word:` reference, naming the clip and `frameshell transcribe <asset>`: its times may be wrong. A clip with no transcript holds no words to count: a word found elsewhere resolves, and the refusal of an unknown word, or of `#n` past the last time, names such clips with the command. Decision (open for the owner to revisit): heard clips without a transcript are skipped, not refused, because a music or effects track has none and would otherwise block every `word:`; the cost is that `#n` counts only transcribed speech, so speech on an untranscribed second camera is not counted and no warning says so on success. An unknown word lists up to five closest played words (`editDistance`, with how often each is said); `#n` past the last time lists the times; a timeline that plays no transcribed word is refused, its hint naming `transcribe` and that words inside nested timeline clips are not read.
- **Where it runs.** The grid's pattern: `resolveTimeRef` throws `WordsNeeded` while the scope has no words, and `resolveTimeRefAsync` takes an optional `WordLoader` after the grid loader, loads and tries again; a caller without one refuses plainly. One load serves every field of a request or call (`onceWords`). `TimelineService` takes `wordsOf` (the daemon's, from `readTimelineWords`, stale check by `media.hash`) and builds the loader from the timeline the operation sees, so a cut earlier in the transaction counts and a review-mode proposal resolves against its own timeline. `snapRequest` (`--snap-to`) takes the loader too. Every argument that takes a TimeRef reads words: the operations, `captions.set`, `multicam.direct`, `ui.seek`, `sound.place`, `sound.music`, `voiceover.place`, `music.fit`, `music.shape`, `music.candidates`, batch slots and `script.outline` times. Not yet: the review `requirements.json` `appearsBy` (a sync resolver, whose description names seconds and markers only), which refuses `word:` as a caller that cannot read words.
- **Journal**: seconds, as for markers and beats. After a cut the same name can mean a later word, but an operation already recorded never moves (pinned by a test that resolves one name before and after a `cut`).
- **Wire**: the `TimeRef` description names the form (model-facing); argument types unchanged. Protocol 170.

### Decision 8 as built (timing for generated clips)

- **Opt-in per clip, decided by the adapter.** `ClipAdapter.timing(clip, context)` (plugin API, optional, so `apiVersion` stays "1") returns the kinds the clip's render reads: `beats` (the grid with its bar starts) and `markers`. It is called on every key check, like `inputs`; when it throws or returns no array the clip has no key and is unavailable, with the reason, like a failing `inputs`. Absent, empty or only unknown words: the clip gets nothing and its key is the one it had, so moving the music, changing the tempo or moving a marker re-renders no other clip.
- **`RenderContext.timing`** (and `LiveContext.timing`, so the live page shows what the render will): `beats` as `{ at, beat, bar? }`, `tempo` `{ bpm, meter }` and `markers` as `{ at, id, label, color? }`, only those that fall inside the clip (half a frame of slack at its edges, as the grid's own), in **composition seconds** (`in + timeline second - start`, 6 decimals). `beat` and `bar` are the numbers `beat:N` and `bar:N` use (slice 4: counted in the music file, so a head trim of the music does not renumber). A grid `--snap-to` would refuse gives `beats: []` and `beatsProblem`, never a failed render: a clip that syncs to music still renders before the music is measured. `beatsProblem` is a stable code, `no-tempo`, `no-music-clip` (the tempo's clip is not a media clip), `no-beats` (no beats file yet), `broken-beats` (the file cannot be read or does not validate) or `low-confidence`, as `unusableGrid` classifies it for `--snap-to` too. The code enters the key, never the refusal's words: those carry a file path, a confidence or a parser message, which would re-render the clip while the reason holds. `RENDER_BEATS_PROBLEMS` (plugin API) words each code for an adapter or composition that shows it.
- **One grid, computed in core** (`packages/core/src/clips/timing.ts`, `clipTiming`): `tempoGrid` of the timeline the clip sits in, read through the daemon's `beatsOf`, as `tempo.show` and the TimeRef resolver read it. A clip inside a nested timeline is keyed in the flattened timeline, so it hears the host's music and markers (decision 5 keeps the nested file's own markers inside it). Background refresh keys it the same way (`#refreshOnce` flattens every timeline file), and skips the timing clips of a file another timeline nests: keyed against that file alone it would render a second time, for a timing no preview or export of the host uses. `clip.renders` of the nested file itself still keys against it, on demand; `gc` keeps both keys while both files exist. A test pins that export keys a timing clip, top-level or nested, as `clip.renders` does.
- **Cache key.** `ClipKeyInput.timing` is the whole timing object; absent drops out of the canonical form like `style` and `motionBlur`, so existing keys are unchanged (pinned by test) and `CACHE_LAYOUT_VERSION` stays 1. Moving `beatsProblem` from words to codes changed only the keys of clips that asked and had no usable grid: their old entries are never looked up again and `gc` sweeps them, so the version stays 1 there too. For a clip that asks, the key also changes when the clip itself moves or is trimmed against the grid: its render shows different beats then. The clip renderer's file watcher already sees `beats/` edits and the daemon refreshes on every timeline change, so a new grid queues the renders it affects.
- **HyperFrames.** A composition asks with `<meta name="frameshell-timing" content="beats markers">` in its entry (spaces or commas, any case; the entry is an input, so editing the tag re-keys the clip anyway). The adapter puts `<script data-frameshell-timing>window.frameshellTiming = {…}</script>` first in the head of the render copy, and the same assignment first in the live page's `head`, escaped so no label can close the script. The project file is never changed.
- **Wire.** None: timing travels inside the render and the live page's `head`; `PROTOCOL_VERSION` unchanged.
- **Motion runtime helpers (built, runtime 0.5.0, slice MH of #458).** `packages/motion/src/beats.ts` reads `window.frameshellTiming` through pure functions of `t`, so a seek paints the same picture whatever was painted before: `beats(nominal?)`, `beatAt(t, nominal?)` (`{ index, beat, bar, phase }`), `beatTime(n)`, `sinceBeat(t, n)` (the pure form of `onBeat(n, fn)`: no callbacks, a paint has no event to hang one on), `springOnBeat(preset, t, n, from?, to?)`, `markers(label?)`, `hasBeats()` and `frameshellTiming()`. `n` counts the clip's own beats from 0; before the first and after the last the grid continues at the nearest period (the tempo's for a clip with one beat), because a clip rarely holds the beat a move should start on. `bar` is the number `bar:N` uses, counted from any bar start inside the clip, and null when none is. With no usable grid (no meta tag, `beatsProblem` set, empty beats) every helper uses `nominal` (`{ bpm, offset?, meter?, duration? }`, beat `n` at `offset + n * 60 / bpm`), and without one the clip's own seconds (60 BPM from 0), so a composition passes the pace its props already say and looks the same without music; `markers` has no fallback and is `[]`. The `checklist` template is the reference: the `onBeats` prop puts its ticks on the first beats at or after `tickStart`, with `tickStart` and `tickGap` as the nominal grid. The prop itself asks for the beats ("Words in timing as built"); a meta tag scaffolded into the entry would key every checklist on the music. Existing clips keep their frozen runtime (ADR 0033) until `hyperframes update`.
### Remotion binding as built (slice RB of #458)

Owner decision 2026-11-18 (Q6 of #458). A Remotion entry is a bundle, not HTML, so the `<meta>` tag of the HyperFrames binding has no counterpart; the clip itself asks.

- **Opt-in: clip prop `timing`.** `props.timing: ["beats", "markers"]` (`propsSchema`; any list of those two words, `[]` asks for nothing). Unknown words, or a value that is not a list, are refused on `clip.add`/`clip.set` with the allowed words. `timing(clip)` of the adapter returns exactly those kinds (sorted, once each), synchronously, with no file read. A clip without the prop, or with `[]`, returns none, so its key is the one it had; the prop itself is part of `props`, so adding it to a clip re-keys that clip once, like any props change.
- **Data: reserved input prop `frameshellTiming`.** `render` and `livePreview` put `ctx.timing` (`RenderTiming`, as is: `beats`, `tempo`, `beatsProblem`, `markers`, composition seconds) into the input props under that key, merged after the clip's own `inputProps`. The same merged object goes to `selectComposition`, `renderMedia` and the live page's `remotion_inputProps`, so `calculateMetadata`, the render and the live page see one value. The merge applies only when the host passed a timing, which it does only for the kinds the clip asked for; a clip that did not ask gets its `inputProps` object untouched. Composition seconds are `frame / fps` of the composition (the render keeps the composition's length in seconds).
- **Reserved key rule: refused, while the clip asks.** A clip that lists `timing` and also sets `inputProps.frameshellTiming` is refused by `propsSchema` with a pointer to remove one of them (the host's data would silently replace the person's). A clip that does not ask keeps whatever it has in `inputProps` under that name; nothing is merged into it.
- **Key.** No second key path. The core already adds `ClipKeyInput.timing` for a clip whose adapter returns kinds, exactly as for HyperFrames; `CACHE_LAYOUT_VERSION` stays 1 and existing Remotion clips keep their keys (pinned by test: `timing()` returns none for them). `beatsProblem` reaches the composition as the same stable code.
- **Reading it.** `frameshell remotion new` also writes `src/frameshell-timing.ts` into the Remotion project when missing (never overwrites): the types of the data and `useFrameshellTiming()`, which reads `useVideoConfig().props.frameshellTiming` (null when the clip did not ask). It holds no maths: the beat maths of `packages/motion/src/beats.ts` read `window.frameshellTiming` and are not shared, so a composition compares `frame / fps` with `beats[i].at` itself (a `beatAt` twin is a follow-up, only if it can import the one in `packages/motion`). Projects created before this slice add the file by hand (the skill shows its one line) or read `getInputProps().frameshellTiming`.
- **Wire.** None. `PROTOCOL_VERSION` unchanged, no plugin API change.
- Not in this part: a Remotion `beatAt` twin. Words came with the next slice.

### Words in timing as built (final slice of #458)

Owner decision 2026-11-18 (Q1 of #458): this is the last slice of the ticket.

- **`words` kind.** `RenderTimingKind` gains `words` and `RenderTiming` gains `words` (`RenderWord`: `{ at, end, text, id }`) and `wordsProblem`. The words are those `word:` references count: `readTimelineWords` (the daemon's, stale check by `media.hash`) on the timeline the clip is keyed in, so `placeWords` stays the one placer (unmuted media clips, overlaps trimmed, a word on the clip that plays most of it, words a cut removed gone, `text` as the person corrected it, `id` the transcript's). A word belongs to the clip when its start falls inside it, with the half-frame slack of beats and markers, and both ends map to composition seconds as they do (`end` clamped to the media clip that plays it, not to the generated clip). A clip in a nested timeline is keyed in the flattened timeline, so it hears the host's speech and the nested file's own speech, which the flattening puts on the host; `word:` references still do not read nested speech.
- **`wordsProblem`**, a stable code like `beatsProblem`, refused as `word:` refuses: `stale-transcript` when any heard clip's transcript is older than its file (its times may be wrong), else `no-transcript` when the timeline plays no transcribed word. Either gives `words: []`, never a failed render. Nothing said during the clip, with speech elsewhere, is `words: []` with no code. `RENDER_WORDS_PROBLEMS` (plugin API, next to `RENDER_BEATS_PROBLEMS`) words each code. A transcript file that cannot be parsed makes the clip unavailable with the reason, as a throwing `timing()` does (`cleanup`, `word:` and export verify refuse it too).
- **Wiring.** `ClipRendererOptions.wordsOf(root, timeline, fps)` is the daemon's `wordsOf`, the one `TimelineService` takes, and `ClipTimingInput.wordsOf` reads it only when the clip asks for `words`. The renderer has one key path (`#keyOf`), so preview, export, `clip.renders` and background refresh all pass it, as they pass `beatsOf`. The transcripts are read on every key check of an asking clip and only then; the project watcher already sees `transcripts/` edits, so a corrected word queues the renders it affects.
- **Key.** The key counts the whole timing object, so there is no new key path and `CACHE_LAYOUT_VERSION` stays 1. A clip that asks for words renders again when a word inside it changes, moves (the footage moved, trimmed or re-timed), or when the clip itself moves or is trimmed against the speech; clips that do not ask keep their keys (pinned by test).
- **HyperFrames.** `words` in the `frameshell-timing` meta tag. **Automatic ask:** `timing()` also returns `beats` when the clip's props set the boolean `onBeats` (`ON_BEATS_PROP`) to true, so a template clip asks without a meta tag; `onBeats` false or unset keeps the key, so a plain template never re-renders when the music moves. `checklist`, `notification-stack` (cards drop on beats) and `work-cards` (each card pulses on a beat and lands 0.25 s before it) declare it through `onBeatsVariable`, and share the page-script `stepTimes` in `templates/kit.ts` (the first beat at or after the start, the template's own steps as the nominal grid, so `onBeats` never changes a clip without a usable grid). Runtime unchanged (0.5.0): there is no `M.words()` helper yet; a composition reads `M.frameshellTiming().words`.
- **Remotion.** `"words"` joins `TIMING_KINDS` (refused words now name all three). `src/frameshell-timing.ts` gains `FrameshellWord`, `words` and `wordsProblem`; a parity test holds each interface of the helper source to the keys (and optional marks) of `RenderTiming`, `RenderBeat`, `RenderMarker` and `RenderWord`, through tables `pnpm typecheck` checks against the plugin API types. Projects created earlier add the two fields by hand (the skill shows them).
- **Wire.** None: `PROTOCOL_VERSION` unchanged; the plugin API change is additive and optional, so `apiVersion` stays "1".
- Not in this part: a `M.words()` runtime helper; a Remotion `beatAt` twin.

## Considered options

- **B. Markers plus one grid stored in the timeline file** (rejected): the grid does not follow the music clip, so moving or trimming the music silently desyncs every beat; hundreds of measured beats land in every inverse patch; a second timeline re-measures.
- **C. Beats as markers** (`kind: beat`, rejected): hundreds of markers bloat history and proposals, beats do not follow the music, and there is no place for confidence or half/double-time correction.
- **D. A learned detector first** (deferred, decision 10): a new pinned model and worker for a gain not measured on our material.
- **Markers fixed on timeline seconds**, as sequence markers in some editors (rejected, decision 1): after a ripple cut they would point at different material.

## Consequences

- An older Frameshell rejects a timeline that carries `markers` (the timeline schema is strict); the CHANGELOG says so.
- The export compiler and preview never read markers: they paint and play nothing.
- Slice 4 is built: seconds, markers, `beat:`, `bar:` and `word:` resolve through one resolver for `transition.set`, `clip.key`, `clip.split`, `cut`, `clip.move`, `clip.trim`, `clip.add`, `marker.*`, `multicam.reframe`, `captions.set`, `multicam.direct`, `sound.place`, `sound.music` and `ui.seek`. Later: `requirements.json` reading words.
