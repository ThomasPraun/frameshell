# Transitions and keyframes

Moves "Transitions and keyframes" from v0.3 to v0.13. A cut may dissolve into the next clip or dip through black, sound crossfades with it, and a clip's position, scale, opacity and gain may change over time. Wipes and slides come later: the preview would need masks or WebGL. Decided by the owner on 2026-10-22.

## Context (verified in the tree)

- Clips on one track are sorted and never overlap (`packages/schema/src/timeline.ts`; engine error "Clips on one track cannot overlap"). `transform` (`x`, `y`, `scale`, `opacity`) and `audio.gain` are static per clip.
- Multicam `reframe` keys (`{ at, x, y }` on the angle's own clock, straight lines, held outside) are the only keyframes. `reframeAt` in `packages/schema/src/composite.ts` is the evaluator; the export twin is the `movingCrop` ffmpeg expression in `packages/core/src/export/compiler.ts`.
- The export compiler concatenates the bottom video track's clips and overlays every upper layer with constant scale and `colorchannelmixer=aa` opacity. Video segments are independent encodes. Audio is one continuous pass with a 2 ms `afade` at every clip edge (`EDGE_FADE_SECONDS`).
- The preview draws one decoder per layer with `globalAlpha` and a constant placement (`program.ts`, `engine.worker.ts`); the audio worklet fades 96 samples at every edge (`mixer.ts`).
- Operation inverses are `timeline.patch` diffs of clips and tracks (ADR 0004), so new clip fields undo with no new code.

**Guiding principle: simple, optional, no required steps.** Every new field is optional with a default, and a project without transitions or keys is unchanged byte for byte (same files, same export plan). There is one way to do each thing: a dissolve needs only a time. Missing material never fails an edit; the edit shortens or falls back and says so in `warnings`. Field shapes stay flat and few, so an agent can write the timeline JSON by hand.

## Decision

1. **Scope.** Dissolve between two clips, fade from or to black (to transparent on upper tracks), audio crossfade and audio fades, keys for `x`, `y`, `scale`, `opacity` and `gain`.
2. **A transition belongs to a clip edge.** Clips stay butted and the no-overlap rule is unchanged. Three optional clip fields, seconds: `dissolve` on the incoming clip (mix from the clip that ends where it starts on its track), `fadeIn` and `fadeOut` (from and to nothing). On an audio track, or for the sound of a clip on a video track, a dissolve is a crossfade and a fade goes to silence. Names avoid the media `in`/`out`.
3. **Centered on the cut.** A dissolve of length d runs from the cut minus d/2 to the cut plus d/2: the outgoing clip plays on past its `out`, the incoming clip starts before its `in`. Those extra source frames are the **handles**. The timeline length never changes. A fade lies inside its clip.
4. **Effective length, computed in one place.** The file stores the requested length; one schema function (`edgeTransitions` in `packages/schema/src/transitions.ts`) returns what plays, and export, preview and engine warnings all call it. Each half of a dissolve is cut to the handle both clips have and to half of each clip; halves are whole frames. With no handle on either side, the dissolve plays as a dip through black (fade out, then fade in, over the clips' own frames). A dissolve whose clip no longer starts where another ends (after a move, remove or trim) plays as a fade in of the same length; the field is kept, so butting the clips again restores it. Any edge takes at most half its clip. Operations that leave a transition shorter or changed add a `warnings` entry; nothing fails.
5. **Handles per clip type.** Media: the source before `in` and after `out` (divided by `speed`). Still images: unlimited. Adapter clips: the render is clip-length and its cache key stays as it is, so the first or last rendered frame is held (compositions usually start and end at rest). Nested timelines: the nested timeline's own seconds outside the clip's window.
6. **Keyframes.** `keys` on a clip holds one sorted list per property: `{ at, value, ease? }`. `at` is on the clip's own clock, the seconds its `in` counts (media source seconds, composition seconds, nested-timeline seconds), as `reframe` keys are on the group clock. Keys then stay on the same frames through trim, split, ripple `cut` and speed changes with no rewriting. Between keys the value follows the leaving key's `ease`: `linear` (default), `in` (f²), `out` (f(2−f)) or `in-out` (f²(3−2f)); no free curves. Before the first key and after the last the value holds. A property with keys ignores its static value (`transform` field, `audio.gain`); without keys the static value plays. `gain` is in dB; `muted` still wins. Audio tracks take only `gain` keys, as they take no `transform`.
7. **Operations.** `transition.set { at, kind?, duration?, tracks? }`: `kind` `dissolve` (default) or `fade`, `duration` default 0.5 s, `at` a clip edge in timeline seconds. By default it applies to that edge on every video and audio track that has one, as `cut` does; `tracks` limits it. Where two clips butt, `dissolve` sets the incoming clip's `dissolve` and `fade` sets half the length as `fadeOut` and `fadeIn` (a dip); on a lone edge both kinds set a fade. `transition.remove { at, tracks? }` clears them. `clip.key { clip, prop, time, value?, ease?, remove?, clear? }` adds or replaces the key at the frame of timeline second `time` (value default: what plays there now), removes it, or clears the property. Split gives the left part `dissolve`/`fadeIn`, the right part `fadeOut`, the new cut none; both keep every key. Protocol bump, three registry entries, MCP tools from the registry, skill updated.
8. **Audio crossfade is equal-power.** Both sides follow a quarter sine (ffmpeg `qsin`), so material that does not correlate (two takes, music into voice) keeps its loudness through the middle. Linear dips there by 3 dB, and the edits that ask for a crossfade are mostly between different material; a hard cut inside one take keeps the 2 ms edge fade. Audio fades use the same curve.
9. **One evaluator, pinned twins.** `composite.ts` gains `placementAt(clip, t)`, `gainAt(clip, t)` and the transition weights. Bottom track: the incoming clip is drawn over the outgoing one with opacity w (0 to 1), an exact mix with no dip. Upper tracks: the outgoing clip rides 1−w and the incoming w, so the layers below show through as the overlay changes. A fade multiplies opacity, black under the bottom track. Nested clips compose at each time (`composePlacement`: offsets and scale compose, opacity multiplies, gain adds); a nested clip's edge transitions reach its flattened clips on the window's edges, a dissolve on its own track and fades on its extra layers and sound. Export writes the same formulas as ffmpeg expressions of timeline time, as `movingCrop` does: `overlay` x/y with `eval=frame`, `scale` with `eval=frame`, alpha ramps (`fade=alpha=1` for linear spans, an alpha expression for eased keys), `volume` with `eval=frame` for gain and `afade` `qsin` for fades and crossfades. A dissolve on the bottom track is the incoming piece overlaid on the outgoing clip's extended tail, not `xfade`, so every track follows one layer model. Segments may still cut inside a transition: every expression reads timeline time from the piece's start. A parity test samples the evaluator against the expressions; golden plans pin the graphs.
10. **Preview.** `program.ts` places the incoming clip of a dissolve on a second slot of its track for the overlap, so a track with a dissolve gets one more decoder; read-ahead primes it from the handle. The worker draws each frame with the evaluated placement and opacity. The audio plan gives each span a gain envelope (keys, fades, crossfades) that the mixer applies per sample.
11. **Multicam.** Shots get no transitions or keys for now; program tracks keep hard cuts, and `transition.set` skips them with a warning. The fields are clip-edge properties, so shots can take them later without a model change.
12. **UI, basic.** The timeline marks an edge with a transition at its effective length; right-click on an edge adds a dissolve or fade or removes it; the inspector edits kind and duration. Keys show as diamonds on the clip; the inspector has an "add keyframe at playhead" button per property, an ease choice and a remove. Image projects show neither. Dragging keys and a keyframe lane come later.

## Considered options

- **Overlapping clips on one track** (the A/B roll of older editors). Rejected: it breaks the invariant that engine, schema, export and preview rely on, and every ripple operation would need overlap rules.
- **A transition list on the track** (`transitions: [{ at, … }]`). Rejected: it goes stale when clips move or ripple, so every operation would have to rewrite it; a field on the clip moves with the clip.
- **Fail when handles are short.** Rejected: an agent would need a second attempt for every dissolve on tight edits (case B cuts at pauses with little to spare).
- **Keys in clip seconds from `start`.** Rejected: a head trim or a ripple cut would slide every animation against its picture.
- **`xfade` for bottom-track dissolves.** Rejected: bottom-track only, and a second path to keep in parity with the preview.
- **Bézier curves per key.** Rejected: hard to hand-edit and to state as an ffmpeg expression; four eases cover the use cases.

## Consequences

- Schema: optional `dissolve`, `fadeIn`, `fadeOut` and `keys` on video and audio clips; JSON Schema regenerated. A timeline without them is unchanged.
- Protocol v51: `transition.set`, `transition.remove`, `clip.key`; CLI `transition set|remove`, `clip key`; skill updated.
- `--verify` may hear a word from a handle under a crossfade; a 0.5 s crossfade at a snapped cut rarely holds one.
- Export of animated opacity costs more per frame than a constant; only keyed and transition pieces pay it.
- Not covered: wipes, slides and other shaped transitions, transitions between shots, keys on crop or rotation, speed ramps.
